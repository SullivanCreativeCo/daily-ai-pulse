Ken Griffin says we're almost certainly in a bubble.

His reason is simple. Companies are opening their Anthropic and OpenAI bills and asking where the productivity is.

I can't tell you if he's right about the market.

But I know that question already.

Every small business owner I've worked with has asked it since day one. Not in those words. More like, "Is this going to save me an afternoon or not?"

Big companies are just now learning to ask it.

That's the good news for anyone building this stuff for small shops. The bar was never "look how smart the model is." The bar was always "what did it take off my plate this week?"

Would someone pay for that? I think someone would pay for that.

So here's my filter. If I can't point at the hours it gave back, I don't ship it.

What's the one AI thing in your business you could point at and say it gave you time back?
LI . NO. 02
Here's math nobody puts on the agent demo slide.

Say an AI agent gets each step right 95% of the time. That sounds great.

Now give it ten steps.

It finishes without a single mistake about 60% of the time.

Twenty steps? Closer to a third.

That's why the flashy "it does everything on its own" demos fall apart on real work. Small errors stack.

The fix isn't a smarter agent. It's shorter jobs with a check in between.

Research 50 prospects, then a person looks at the list. Fix the image descriptions on 40 product pages, with a backup first. Draft the reply, then you hit send.

If you can write the steps down in advance, you don't need an agent at all. You need a workflow with one AI step in it. Cheaper, faster, easier to trust.

Boring wins here.

What's one job you've been trying to hand to an agent that would honestly work better as a plain workflow?
LI . NO. 03
Three different companies shipped agent products this week.

Sakana. Oracle. A Swedish startup called Strawberry.

Different buyers, different pitch, same design choice.

They all put a human approval step inside the process.

Sakana writes it right into the workflow. The agent reads the supplier reply, analyzes it, then waits for a person to approve before anything runs. Strawberry's agents pause before they send an email or change a record.

That's not a safety bolt-on. That's the product.

And it's the part small business owners care about most. They don't want a robot with the keys to the bank account. They want something that does the grunt work and says, "Hey, look at this before I send it."

The sales pitch used to be "it runs by itself."

The pitch that actually closes is "it does the work, you keep the say."

Where in your business would you want that pause button?
