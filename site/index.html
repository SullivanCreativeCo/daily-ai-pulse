LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
A model that performs on par with the flagship at a tenth of the price per token just shipped.

OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Terra is $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output. They say it's about 3x faster than Astra, too.

Those are OpenAI's numbers on OpenAI's own tests. They still recommend the bigger models for the hardest coding. I'd test it before I believe it.

But here's why I care. Most of what a small business wants automated isn't hard. It's follow-ups, intake, sorting, summaries. Boring, repeatable stuff that was never worth flagship pricing.

When the mid-tier model gets this good, the question changes. It's not 'can AI do this?' anymore. It's 'what did I shelve because the bill didn't pencil out?'

I've got a list like that. Probably you do too.

What's the workflow you killed because the token math didn't work?
LI . NO. 02
Somebody on a podcast said give away the CRM.

Greg Isenberg's argument is that building the software got so easy that the tool itself isn't what people pay for anymore. So give the tool away, get people in, then charge for the intelligence on top. Agents doing the work. Or a human who does it for you.

Think about a calorie app. Tracking is free. A personalized meal plan costs money. A weekly coach costs more.

Now think about a local business. What's the thing they pay for just to feel organized? The scheduling tool, the follow-up tool, the review tool?

They don't actually want the tool. They want the weight gone.

That's not just a service, that's a weight removal product.

So what's the tool in your industry people pay for, that they'd happily never open again?
LI . NO. 03
The best part of the AWS Step Functions news is a refund.

They just made existing workflows available as tools for AI agents. The agent decides when to run one and what to pass in. Then the workflow runs every step exactly the way you built it.

Their example is a support agent starting a refund. The eligibility check, the manager approval and the credit all happen in the same order, every time.

That's the split I keep coming back to. Let the agent make the judgment call. Don't let it improvise the steps that touch money.

Same idea for a solo business. An agent that reads your inbox probably shouldn't also be the one paying your bills.

Which step in your business should never be improvised?

New harnesses & tools

  • Droid 1.0 - Factory The big change is programmatic tool calling. The agent writes a script that makes the tool calls, so one model request can run hundreds of them and the raw file dumps never clog its context. Factory says Droid beats the native harnesses built for each model by up to 20% on efficiency. The harness is model-independent, and an engineer reviews every change before it ships. Builder takeaway: the harness is where the quality and the cost live now, not just the model. https://factory.com/news/frontier-harness

  • GitHub Agentic Workflows is GA - AI and Tech News You write a workflow in plain Markdown, it compiles to Actions YAML, and a coding agent handles things like issue triage and test writing. Agents are read-only by default and sandboxed behind a firewall. The default engine needs a Copilot license. GitHub's team numbers (91% AI-authored commits) are self-reported. Why you'd care: it's a cheap way to see what "agent as teammate" looks like without building your own harness. https://www.aiandtech.news/article/github-agentic-workflows-is-generally-available

  • Microsoft's Copilot harness in Foundry - The New Stack Public preview, with sandboxing, skills, MCP and subagents handled for you. Free until December 1, then $0.0994 per vCPU-hour and $0.0118 per GiB-hour. The trade is real. You pick the harness when you create the agent and can't change it later, and you can't bring your own model. Easy until you want to leave. https://thenewstack.io/microsoft-copilot-harness-foundry/
