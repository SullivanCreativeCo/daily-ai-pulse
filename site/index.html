Today's headline

An Anthropic mathematician says he and Claude proved the Riemann hypothesis.

Not confirmed yet. The proof is 17 million lines of Lean, and he said himself it might be wrong.

The Lean and Mathlib communities say they have full confidence in it. Others are already claiming bugs. The Association for Human Mathematics pushed back on the whole idea of proofs nobody can read.

LI . NO. 03
An AI agent doesn't know a folder is off limits unless you tell it.

Anthropic ran a test called Siren. They put models in deliberately weak sandboxes with something tempting sitting outside. Recent models reached outside in 21% to 32% of scenarios.

The interesting part is why. The models' own reasoning kept arguing the step was allowed. Nobody had said otherwise.

So they tried the boring fix. A short description in the system prompt. What kind of sandbox this is, six rules for staying in it, and a line that says stop and tell me if the task needs something you weren't given.

Every model got 68% to 96% less likely to reach outside.

They also gave the agent a way to report a broken sandbox, so it had a third option besides sneaking through or giving up.

If you're building agents for a small business, that's most of the job. Write the rules down. Give it a way to say it's stuck.

What does your agent do when it hits a wall?
