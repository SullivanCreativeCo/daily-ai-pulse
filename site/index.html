Today's headline

OpenAI shipped GPT-6 Cyber, and they rated it "Critical" on their own cybersecurity scale. Last version was "High."

It found 948 privilege escalation exploits in Linux and 81 new high-severity bugs in Firefox, a codebase that's been picked over by every frontier model already.

The part to sit with is the other number. It stayed inside its assigned scope 99.93% of the time without a safety prompt. Capability is the headline, but what the model won't do is the product.

LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
A 60-year-old who calls himself "not a tech guy" says he's making $25 to 30K a month putting an AI helper on small business websites.

That's his claim on a Koerner Office episode, so take the number with a grain of salt.

But look at where it started. His phone repair shop.

Same questions, all day. "I have this model of phone, my screen's cracked. What's it cost, how fast, am I gonna lose my data?"

He wrote those answers into an AI on his own site. A few hundred visits a month turned into about 130 AI conversations. Around 60 of those booked or left a number.

He wasn't hunting for a clever idea. He was tired of answering the same three questions.

What are you already answering for yourself that someone else would find useful?

Your ideas aren't bad ideas. They're just not finished ideas. Sometimes the unfinished part is just writing down what you say out loud every day.
LI . NO. 02
Here's a number that stuck with me this week.

A small business directory counted 159 AI tools. 52 of them, about a third, show no price at all.

Book a demo. Get a quote. You know the drill.

And the ones that do show a price have their own tricks. The middle monthly price was $38, but plenty of it is per user, and some of those numbers only apply if you pay a year up front.

A $15 tool for a team of six is $90 a month. Nobody puts that on the big pricing banner.

This is a vendor counting their own listings, so it's a snapshot, not gospel.

But the move is simple. Before the demo ends, ask for the monthly cost for your team size, in writing.

The no hurts less when you're not emotionally invested. Ask the price before you fall in love with the demo.
LI . NO. 03
Two launches this week looked totally different. Same lesson underneath.

Anthropic shipped Personal Finance in Claude. It connects to your bank and answers money questions. Then they led with what it can't do. Read-only. Can't move your money. No ads.

OpenAI shipped GPT-6 Cyber, a model that found 948 Linux exploits. And the number they pushed hardest was staying in scope. It avoided out-of-scope targets 99.93% of the time without a safety prompt.

Both companies treated the limits as part of the product.

If you're setting up an AI for a small business, that's the template. Start it read-only. Let it draft before it sends. Make it show a card and wait for a yes.

Trust gets built by what the thing won't do.

So what's the one action you'd never want your AI to take without asking?
