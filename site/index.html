LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
ChatGPT can pay for things now.

OpenAI launched Wallet. Your agent finds the item, you check the total, tap Face ID, and it's done. The merchant never sees your real card number.

It's Pro users in the US only for now. But the part I keep chewing on is the playbook they published for businesses.

Most of it is boring. Make sure your product info is readable. Make sure agents aren't getting blocked at the door. Make sure your payment system doesn't flag a legit agent purchase as fraud.

That's a small business to-do list hiding inside a launch post.

A lot of owners have a site built for a human squinting at a phone. Not for an agent trying to compare three options and buy one.

So here's the question I'd sit with. If an agent showed up to buy from you today, could it?
LI . NO. 02
Every agent launch this week had the same feature.

A human clicks approve.

ChatGPT Wallet needs Face ID on every purchase. Claude for Manufacturing drafts the quote, then stops before anything reaches a customer. GitHub's agentic workflows are read-only by default and can require a reviewer before any write.

Three different companies. Same design choice.

I don't think that's caution. I think that's the product.

The agent does the prep. You keep the decision. That's the whole split.

And it's good news for anybody building this for small businesses. Owners don't need an AI that runs the company. They need one that does the tedious part and hands them a card that says yes or no.

Think about your own week. How much of it is prep work before a ten-second decision?

That prep is the part worth handing off.
LI . NO. 03
Small businesses probably won't buy AI from an AI company.

They'll get it from the IT guy they already trust.

That's the bet behind Hatz AI, which just raised $24M. They don't sell to owners. They sell to MSPs, the managed IT shops, who package it under their own name.

I get it. Most owners don't want to compare a dozen chatbots. They don't know which employee pasted client data into which tool. They want one person to call.

But it also means AI is about to show up as a line item on an IT invoice, and a lot of owners won't know what to ask.

Where does our data go? Who can see what? What's actually automated, and who catches the mistakes?

If your provider can't answer those in plain English, that tells you something.

Who's the person your business already trusts with the keys?

New harnesses & tools

  • Dynamic Workflows in Antigravity - Google, GEroundtable Type /run-workflow and the agent writes a Python script that coordinates parallel and sequential subagents. It shows you the script, waits for approval, then runs in the background. You can save the .py file and rerun it later. Builders get a reviewable, repeatable artifact instead of a long chat nobody can reproduce. It's live for AI Pro and Ultra. https://geroundtable.com/antigravity-dynamic-workflows/

  • GitHub Agentic Workflows is generally available - GCN You describe the automation in plain Markdown and it compiles to Actions YAML. It covers issue triage, CI failure analysis, docs and tests. Agents are read-only by default, can be gated behind a reviewer, and run in a sandbox behind a firewall. Builders get a safe way to put an agent in a real repo without handing it the keys. https://gcn.com/github-agentic-workflows-open-source-tool/23243/

  • Synadia Agent Fabric (public preview) - Synadia This is plumbing so agents on different models and harnesses can find and call each other, with signed prompts, per-agent keys and tracing across the whole chain. The preview is free, hosted, and not for production. It matters once you have more than a couple of agents and nobody is in the loop to catch a bad handoff. https://www.synadia.com/blog/synadia-agent-fabric-preview
