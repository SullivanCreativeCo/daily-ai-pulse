Google just made its task-doing Gemini agent free for hundreds of millions of people.

Not a demo. Not a waitlist. US, Japan, Korea, Canada, Australia, India.

It can call local businesses, pay through Google Pay, take forwarded emails, and work in the background while you're stuck in a meeting. You flip a toggle from Chat to Task.

Most small shops built their whole front door around a human picking up. Or a voicemail nobody checks.

I don't think that's scary. I think it's kinda the opening.

If an assistant called your shop tomorrow, would it get a real answer?
LI . NO. 02
Nobody wakes up wanting a GitHub repo.

Corey Ganim walked through three open source repos this week, and the interesting part isn't the repos. It's what he does with them.

One watches how an owner spends their week and ranks the top five time sinks. One checks what ChatGPT and Claude say when someone asks for a business like theirs.

Neither one builds anything. The report is the product. The fix comes after, if they want it.

That's the whole model. The owner doesn't want a tool. They want to know where the week is leaking.

And "find out" is a way easier yes than "install this."

Would someone pay for this? I think someone would pay for this.

What's the thing in your business you'd pay $999 just to see clearly?
LI . NO. 03
Every enterprise software company shipped an agent builder this week.

Microsoft IQ. Salesforce's Coworker, free inside Starter and Pro. Vercel's eve 1.0. Fountain opened up Orchestra. Genesys launched standalone agents.

Different logos, same pitch. Describe the job, wire the tools, approve the result.

Microsoft said it almost straight out: every company has access to the same frontier models. What's different is everything around the model. How your team works. What you've already decided. Where the answers live.

Which means the agent isn't the edge anymore. The agent is getting cheap.

The edge is whether anything useful is written down for it to read.

Most small businesses keep that stuff in one person's head.

If your best person took a week off, could an agent cover Monday?

# Daily AI Pulse - 2026-11-18

## Today's headline
Google made Gemini's task agent free for hundreds of millions of people.

It calls local businesses, pays through Google Pay, and takes forwarded email. It works in the background while you're in a meeting. The free tier covers six countries, and the fancier stuff starts in the US.

The part to sit with: a customer's assistant is about to do the phoning and the shopping. Your front door might need to talk to software.

## AI launches & news
- **GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol** - OpenAI
  OpenAI shipped two models. Astra is the frontier one. Sol is the everyday one. The pitch is better results with fewer tokens, so you get more done inside your plan.
  Astra's API price is 20% lower than GPT-6 Astra's, at $8 in and $40 out per million tokens. Sol keeps the old Sol price. On OpenAI's own agent benchmark, Astra beats GPT-6 Astra at under a third of the cost. That's their number, so grain of salt. But cost per task is the story now, not just smarter.
  <https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-2-astra-sol/>

- **Anthropic's IPO is this week** - AI Daily Brief
  First trade is Thursday the 19th, with pricing the day before. Reports say the raise is $57-64B from IPO buyers plus about $26B in private placement, and the book was around 7x oversubscribed heading into the weekend. NLW also says Wall Street is struggling to value it.
  That's Scott Galloway on Pivot, arguing the first trade pops 15-40% over pricing.
  <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X8Nfe1smEYI>


> "Anthropic can't have a broken IPO."

- **Businesses in ChatGPT and Business Hub** - CB Herald, via MakeAndUpdate
  OpenAI launched a beta on Nov 10 that lets a business claim its profile in ChatGPT. Once verified, you can update details, add product feeds, and see mentions and website clicks. OpenAI's help center says claiming doesn't change your visibility. Agents are only for eligible US ecommerce and online service businesses for now.
  Small agencies are already selling "we'll claim it for you." The service is real, but the claim itself is free and doesn't promise anything yet.
  <https://cbherald.com/makeandupdate-launches-gpt-business-to-help-small-businesses-claim-their-business-in-chatgpt/>

- **SenseNova Loop-9B** - SenseTime
  A 9B model that runs its shared layers twice to get deeper compute from fewer parameters. The 4-bit weights are about 4.9GB and run in under 10GB of GPU memory. SenseTime says it's competitive with Qwen3.8-27B on agent and data analysis tests, but it still fumbles complex tool definitions.
  Good one to watch if you've got a client who wants AI on their own hardware.
  <https://www.sensetime.com/en/news/sensenova-loop-9b-release-20261118-1435>

## Enterprise AI moves
- **Microsoft's seven new MAI models** - Microsoft AI
  The headline is MAI Code 2, which Microsoft says gets 98% of GPT-6.1 Sol's coding quality at about 40% of the price. The range goes from cyber to image to realtime voice at $0.01 per audio minute. They also announced MAI Licensed Weights, so a company can customize a frontier model and run it in its own Azure.
  These are Microsoft's benchmarks, and they say Code 2 isn't tuned for the hardest or longest tasks. Still, the direction is clear: cheap enough to run agents all day.
  <https://microsoft.ai/news/microsoft-ignite-2026/>

- **Agentforce Coworker is free inside Starter and Pro** - Salesforce
  Coworker is on by default once you import your data. It summarizes an account, moves an opportunity to the next stage, drafts the follow-up, and logs the activity. It keeps a human in the loop and respects permissions.
  The line that matters is "no additional cost." Small teams that never learned CRM now have a coworker explaining the CRM. That's a different thing to compete with.
  <https://www.salesforce.com/agentforce/small-business/coworker-demo/>

- **InMobi Accelerate** - InMobi
  Seven agents (analyst, audience, planner, copywriter, designer, launcher, optimizer) run campaigns across Google, Meta, Microsoft, TikTok and ChatGPT Ads from one dashboard. It's free to start. InMobi's own survey says 93% of SMB advertisers can't confidently measure performance across platforms, and only 4% can launch same-day.
  Nothing goes live without approval at launch. Worth watching if you sell marketing to small shops.
  <https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261118726828/en/InMobi-Launches-InMobi-Accelerate-an-Agentic-AI-Advertising-Platform-for-Growing-Businesses-and-Agencies>


> "A growing business should be able to run a disciplined advertising program without hiring seven specialists first." - Rohit Dosi, InMobi

## New harnesses & tools
- **eve 1.0** - Vercel
  An open-source framework for production agents. You pick the model and tools, and it saves progress so an agent can pick up after a crash. It has multiplayer sessions with per-person approvals, and native channels like Slack, Teams, Discord and iMessage. Vercel says Rillet tripled its shipping rate in three months with a software factory on it.
  If you build agents that live in a client's Slack, this saves you from hand-rolling the approval plumbing.
  <https://vercel.com/blog/eve-is-now-generally-available>

- **Mermaid Flow** - Mermaid
  You draw the workflow as a diagram, connect your tools, and it runs the same steps every time. The model still does the thinking inside each step, but it doesn't get to rewrite the steps. Free plan gives 15 runs a month.
  This is the answer to "my team has five slightly different prompts for triage." One shared copy that anyone can read is a real fix for a small team.
  <https://mermaid.ai/blog/posts/mermaid-flow-the-visual-builder-for-agentic-workflows>

- **Orchestra** - Fountain
  Describe a job in plain words and get a working agent back, with triggers, connections and limits wired. Fountain says it runs about 80 of its own agents across every function. Some of it is live today, and the event-triggered and human-handoff parts are staged over the next few weeks. They published the sequence instead of pretending it's finished, which is rare.
  It's frontline-ops focused, but the "agents propose themselves from what they watch" idea is worth stealing.
  <https://www.fountain.com/posts/introducing-orchestra>
