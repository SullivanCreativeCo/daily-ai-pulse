LI . NO. 03
Someone at Anthropic asked a model where the NIST elliptic curve seeds came from. Under half an hour later, it had an answer.

People have wondered about this for years. The rumor was that the seeds were a hash of a joke phrase. Several people at Anthropic chased that and got nowhere.

Claude dropped the rumor. It guessed how a 1990s program would have made random numbers, scanned the seeds for that fingerprint, and found it. An old C library generator, started from the clock.

No backdoor. No attack. Anthropic says it makes them more confident the curves are clean.

Here's what I keep thinking about. The win wasn't a smarter answer. It was a different question. Stop looking for the phrase. Ask how the machine that made it would have worked.

How would I fix this? Sometimes that starts with how it was built in the first place.

AI launches & news

  • Meta Muse Spark Open - The Guardian, MarkTechPost Meta released an 816B open-weight model with a 1M context window. Alexandr Wang called it the most capable US open-weight model to date. It's still huge. The full version needs about a terabyte of memory, so two top-end Mac Studios, around $40,000. Apple even updated MLX to support it. https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/oct/16/meta-releases-muse-spark-open-a-new-open-weight-ai-model

"Guys, this is like my Super Bowl. I waited so long for US open weight models to be relevant again." - Nathan Lambert

  • Beam - Reflection, Hugging Face Reflection's first model is a 501B mixture-of-experts with 23B active per token, a 1M context window, and an Apache 2.0 license. It's built for coding and agent work, and it ships with a Rust harness. What I like is the launch. Over 40 partners from day one, including AWS, Cloudflare, Ollama, and OpenRouter. That's the part that makes an open model usable. https://reflection.ai/blog/beam-is-available

  • Step 5 Preview - StepFun, MarkTechPost 600B MoE, 27B active, 1M context, FP8 and BF16 weights are live. The API runs $1.00 in and $2.70 out per million tokens. Its long-context score is the best independent result they listed (88.3%). Its weakest spot is Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 33.3%. https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/16/stepfun-releases-step-5-preview-open-weights-600b-moe-27b-active-1m-context/

  • Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full - Meta, via aiholics Meta's most capable models yet, built for agents. The two features are Active Memory (carries details across conversations) and Active Focus (keeps working when a task gets hard, then checks in before anything important). Per TechCrunch, the API costs $4 in and $20 out per million tokens. Full is for Muse Code subscribers only. The benchmark claims are Meta's own, so wait for outside testing. https://aiholics.com/meta-muse-wonder-muse-wonder-full-agent-models/

  • Gemini 4 Argon - Google Now out to developers, enterprises, and Ultra and Pro subscribers. Launch pricing is $2 in and $10 out per million tokens, and it goes to $4 and $20 after November 30.   • Claude reveals ECDSA curve origins - Anthropic People have wondered for years where the NIST curve seeds came from. The rumor was a hash of a joke phrase, and Anthropic staff chased it without luck. Claude skipped the rumor and guessed how 1990s software would have made random numbers. New harnesses & tools

  • Kiro Workflows - Augmenter Reusable agent recipes with sequential steps, loops, and parallel branches. Each step runs in its own fresh session, so a reviewer doesn't inherit the coder's reasoning. Runs happen in the background across the IDE, CLI, and Web. The bundled recipe plans, codes, runs two independent reviewers, and opens a draft PR. It's opt-in via kiroAgent.workflows.enabled. Why a builder cares: fresh-context reviewers catch what the author talked themselves into. https://augmenter.dev/articles/kiro-workflows-brings-reusable-multi-agent-graphs-to-its-ide-1792133384931/

  • Pi 1.0 and Pi Durable - Today for AI Pi hit 1.0 with native MCP and a new durable framework. If the process crashes, the agent restarts exactly where it left off. Safe-to-replay steps re-run, steps with side effects don't. They also reversed their old stance on MCP and load tools on demand instead of stuffing the whole list into context. Why a builder cares: crash recovery is the boring feature that decides whether an agent can run overnight. https://todayforai.com/en/items/art-64e3d2ac-eb4

"I change my mind when there's new information." - Mario

  • Agent-Native - Builder.io A free, open-source TypeScript framework where agents and the UI share the same actions and state. It's in beta. Why a builder cares: if the agent and the person act on the same data, you write the logic once. https://www.neuralstack.network/article/2026-10-16-builderio-agent-native-typescript-framework-beta

  • Profound's new Agents - Profound You describe the job in plain English, connect tools over MCP, and it runs on a schedule or a trigger. Example triggers: visibility on ChatGPT drops more than 10%, or a competitor publishes. The old node-based version is now "Workflows" and stays. Why a builder cares: the "agent as a briefed teammate" pattern beats a flowchart for jobs that need judgment. https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/introducing-the-next-generation-of-marketing-agents
