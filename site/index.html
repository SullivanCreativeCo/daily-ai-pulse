LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
Claude can now sit in your meeting and start the work before the call ends.

Anthropic put Claude Meetings in beta. It joins Google Meet on Team and Enterprise plans. You ask it for a doc, a timeline, or a number from a connected app, and it drafts while everyone's still talking.

Notice what changed. It's not a note taker. A note taker hands you a summary and you do the work later.

This one makes the first draft in the room.

I haven't tried it yet, so take this as the pitch, not a review. Anthropic's own demo is an event plan, and it's still beta.

But think about a normal client call. The follow-up email, the scope, the rough numbers all happen after you hang up. That's where the real hours go.

If those exist before the call ends, the follow-up gets weird.

If the first draft exists before you hang up, what's the follow-up even for?
LI . NO. 02
The cheap model is the interesting one today.

Alibaba dropped Qwen4-Max. 2.4 trillion parameters, 1M context, and by their own numbers a 62% cut in average cost per task versus the last version. Those are Alibaba's benchmarks, not independent ones.

Then Tencent launched Hy4 flash. $0.07 per million input tokens. It only activates 12 billion parameters at a time, and Tencent says users rated it close to Claude Haiku 5.5 on everyday work like slides and spreadsheets.

Big model gets the headline. Small model changes the math.

For a small business, the question was never which model is smartest. It's what a task costs when you run it 500 times a week.

Do more with the same, not the same with less.

That cost line keeps dropping. When's the last time you looked at what your own automations cost to run?
LI . NO. 03
Your next customer might not have a face.

A company called New Gen just launched Opal. You paste in your website URL, and it turns what your site already does into something an AI assistant can use. Check availability. Ask a follow-up question. Start a booking.

The idea is simple. Getting found by an assistant is step one. Being able to serve the customer it sends is the actual channel.

It's early access, so I'm not calling it yet.

But the question underneath it is real. Most small business websites are built for a person who scrolls. An assistant needs hours, pricing, what's open this week, and how to actually book.

That's usually buried in a PDF or a contact form.

If an AI assistant landed on your site right now, could it book anything?

  • Qwen4-Max - Alibaba, MarkTechPost, Mint Alibaba's new flagship is a 2.4T parameter MoE with 71B active per token, 1M context, and native vision. It's hosted only. The open-weight models are the smaller ones coming later. Alibaba claims a 62% drop in average cost per task versus Qwen3.8-Max. It's $0.07 per million input tokens and $0.281 output, with 224B total parameters but only 12B active, and 1M context. Tencent says it scored close to Claude Haiku 5.5 in an 85-user blind test on everyday work like slides and spreadsheets. It's their own test. Open weights are promised, and it's free on WorkBuddy through Nov 30. https://technode.com/2026/11/16/tencent-launches-hy4-flash-cutting-ai-task-costs-by-95-in-internal-tests/

New harnesses & tools

  • Dynamic Workflows in Antigravity - Google, Code and Chain You type /run-workflow plus a task, and the main agent writes a Python script that coordinates sub-agents. It shows you the script before it runs. So your agent team is a file you can read, version, and hand to a teammate. If you've ever tried to explain what your agents actually did, you get why that matters. https://www.codeandchain.com/en/articles/2026-11-15/article-967ae0ef1a25
