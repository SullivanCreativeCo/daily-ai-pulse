LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
The most interesting thing about Anthropic's new Duet app is the part where it stops.

Duet briefs you every morning. It works in the apps you connect. It runs on its own cloud computer, so your phone doesn't have to stay on.

And before it sends a message, pays for something, or deletes anything, it waits for your OK.

That's the whole design. Do the work, then ask before the part you can't undo.

Anthropic is upfront that it can misread a thread or misjudge what's urgent. Emails it reads can also hide instructions meant to trick it. So the big steps wait for a human.

I think that's the right call. A small business owner doesn't need an agent that moves fast. They need one they can hand the keys to without sweating.

Would you let an agent draft your invoices if you still got to hit send?
LI . NO. 02
Salesforce surveyed over 2,000 people running agentic AI. One finding made me stop.

The teams with stronger governance took longer to reach ROI. 9.3 months versus 7.2.

But they caught problems proactively 68% of the time. The faster group got 55%.

Slower and safer. For a small business, I'd take that trade every time.

And governance doesn't have to mean a committee. For a five-person shop it's one decision: who looks at what the agent did, and when does it hand off to a person?

The fastest returns also came from focus. One narrow job, like sorting support tickets or qualifying leads, with clean data behind it.

Not a company-wide rollout. One job.

Do more with the same, not the same with less. Let the tool clear the routine stuff so your people can do the human part.

What's the one job in your business you'd hand off first?
LI . NO. 03
A guy in Fort Worth says he did about $800,000 in two years fixing brick mailboxes.

He started with $2,000. He kept his day job in medical devices. He spends 15 to 20 hours a week on it.

Nobody wanted those jobs. A $400 mailbox is too small for the big masonry crews. So he took them.

Then the mailboxes got him in with HOAs. HOAs own hundreds of mailboxes. After that came retaining walls and entrances.

That's from Chris Koerner's latest episode, and Koerner says the numbers are self-reported and nobody audited them. Take it as a story, not a spreadsheet.

But look at the shape. Small boring job. Nobody competing for it. One before and after photo that opens a bigger door.

It's not about making millions with one idea. It's about having several profitable little ideas.

What's the job in your industry that's too small for the big guys?
