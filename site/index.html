Daily AI Pulse - 2026-11-03

Today's headline

Google's Gemini 4 Carbon is now the top model on Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index V5. It beats Claude Opus 5.5 by one point and GPT-6 Astra by two.

You can't use it yet. It's in pre-release testing with the U.S. government and a handful of cyber defenders through Google's Fairwind program.

The more useful story today is small business owners getting real hours back with agents. Those run on models you can already use.

LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
A 23-year-old college student is beating most agencies on one thing. Speed.

James Larson walks into a CPA firm and asks for 15 minutes. He records the conversation. An automation hands the transcript to an AI, which drops it into a report template.

By the time he's done talking, the report is in the owner's inbox.

That matters because of what usually happens next. You go away, you do the research, you schedule a second call, and the deal cools off in the gap.

He deleted the gap.

The owner sees the dollar amount next to each opportunity while he's still standing there. No follow-up needed to prove it.

It's a free offer, but it's also a weight removal product. Nobody has to wait for anything.

Would someone pay for that? I think someone would.

Where's the slowest gap in your own process between "yes" and "here it is"?
LI . NO. 02
Google's new model is number one on the leaderboard and you can't use it.

Gemini 4 Carbon beats Claude Opus 5.5 by a single point. It's also in pre-release testing with the U.S. government and a small group of cyber defenders. The rest of us wait.

Meanwhile, a grocery store owner in Iowa photographs his wholesaler's catalog pages and sends them to an AI agent. His weekly ad used to take a couple of hours. Now it takes about 15 minutes.

He's not using the number one model. He's using whatever shipped to him.

That's the thing about this whole race. The benchmark moves one point and the headlines light up. The owner's Thursday gets easier either way.

I'm not saying skip the leaderboard. I'm saying notice which one of these two changed someone's week.

Would you rather have the best model in a locked room, or a good one that already handles your ad?
LI . NO. 03
Greg Isenberg said something on his podcast that stuck with me. The price of software is trending toward zero. You give the software away and upsell agents and human intelligence on top.

The guest, Nick from Orgo, described what that looks like. Take a personal agent platform. Niche it down to one industry, like insurance or dental offices. Pre-build the agents. Sell it for $25,000 upfront and around $3,000 a month to keep it running. Those are his numbers, so take them as his.

The interesting part isn't the price. It's what the business is actually paying for.

Not the app. A generic app is easy to copy.

They're paying for something built around their taste and how their place actually runs, with a person who picks up when it breaks.

Do more with the same, not the same with less.

If the app is free, what's the thing a business still pays you to be around for?

There's a piece going around that says this is the same mistake as old mercantilism, when everyone thought wealth was a fixed pile and the poor couldn't be trusted with freedom.

Then somebody figured out wealth could be created, and ordinary people turned out to be the demand.

I don't know if it maps perfectly. But I keep wondering what we'll find out about everyone else's brains.

Be what you need. Now the software can be, too.

You can run it in the EU, in your own cloud, or on-prem, and you keep the thinking traces and outputs. Their pitch is frontier intelligence without handing control to the model company. New harnesses & tools

  • Factory Droid 1.0 - Lapaas Voice The model writes a JavaScript program that runs many tool calls in a sandbox, instead of going back and forth after every step. Factory says it cut one 15-step workflow from 10 model requests to five. It's model-independent, with desktop apps for macOS and Windows, a terminal interface and an SDK. The benchmark numbers are Factory's own. Builder reason: less back and forth means less context burned and fewer dollars per task. https://lapaasvoice.com/factory-releases-droid-1-0-a-model-independent-ai-coding-agent-built-for-complex-development-tasks/

  • DeepSeek Harness Creator Mode - TAO Media You ask the agent for a tool or workspace and it builds the plugin inside the app. It's open source under MIT, and npx @deepseek-ai/dsh web starts a local version. Builder reason: the app reshapes itself around how you work, which is the same idea as custom builds for clients. https://www.tao.media/deepseek-adds-chat-based-plugin-creation-to-harness-with-creator-mode/

  • DeepSeek Super-Agent - DevOps.com A research prototype, not a product. A Router talks to you and hands work to parallel Worker sessions. DeepSeek says one person can track maybe three to five agent sessions, and this tries to lift that cap. Their internal version picks up a bug reported in chat, tries to reproduce it, and opens a pull request. The article's advice is to treat proactive agents like service accounts, with scoped permissions, logs, and a human check on every PR. https://devops.com/deepseek-wants-coding-agents-to-stop-waiting-for-instructions
