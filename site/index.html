LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
DeepSeek's team said something this weekend that I can't stop thinking about.

One person can keep track of about three to five active AI sessions at a time.

That's the ceiling. Not the model. Not the price. You.

I feel this one. I've had six tabs of Claude going and lost the plot on half of them. The agent was fine. I was the thing that broke.

Their fix is a router and a bunch of workers. You talk to one agent. It hands out the jobs, watches them, and only taps you when something needs a decision.

That's basically a good office manager.

Which means the skill that's gonna matter isn't prompting. It's knowing what deserves your attention and what doesn't.

I'm not sure I'm good at that yet. Most of my day is deciding what I'm gonna ignore.

How many things are you actually tracking right now, and how many of those need you?
LI . NO. 02
A guy named Frey built a list of thrift stores.

That's it. A list.

First month with ads it made $1,200. At its best, $3,000 a month. He says he touches it about 30 minutes every few months.

The part I keep coming back to is the $50 link. He found a competing directory, paid the owner 50 bucks over PayPal, and swapped links. He says that one link got him onto page one, and that the site has made around $150k in four years.

He built it with YouTube and AI because he didn't know how to code.

Here's why it works. He didn't try to be the yellow pages. He picked one narrow thing and saved one kind of person from 20 open tabs.

Every small business owner I know has a version of that. A thing they keep answering the same way, over and over.

What are you already doing for yourself that someone else would find useful?
LI . NO. 03
Meta launched an AI assistant for small business owners. Salesforce shipped a kill switch for agents around the same time.

Different companies, same sentence in the fine print.

Meta says the owner has to approve before Muse publishes, sends, or spends. Salesforce's whole pitch is catching an agent that's quietly giving wrong answers before a customer does.

That tells you where this is at.

The agents can do the work. Nobody fully trusts them to do it alone yet. And the builders know it.

Honestly, I like that. If I'm handing an agent my inbox or my ad budget, I want three things. Let me see what it did. Let me stop it. Let me cap what it spends.

Everything else is a demo.

The business owners I talk to aren't scared of AI. They're scared of waking up to something they didn't approve.

So what would you need to see before you let one run overnight?

New harnesses & tools

  • DeepSeek "Super-Agent" - Global Times One agent you talk to, plus a Router that hands work to Workers and a Watchdog that restarts things when they die. It can spot a bug being discussed in a group chat and start reproducing it without being asked. Open-source prototype, not a product. If you build agent systems, the Router and Workers split is the part to steal. It's the answer to the three-to-five-sessions problem. https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202611/1373392.shtml

  • Factory Droid 1.0 - StreamSage Default mode now has the model write a script that makes lots of tool calls in one go, in a locked-down sandbox. Factory claims lower cost and higher scores than Codex, Cursor and Claude Code on Terminal-Bench 4. All of that is self-benchmarked. Worth a look if your agent loops spend most of their time on tool-call round trips. https://streamsage.news/news/factory-launches-droid-1-0

  • Vercel Labs agent-desktop - the-ai.news Open-source CLI that lets an agent drive desktop apps on Mac, Windows and Linux by reading the accessibility tree instead of taking screenshots. Claims 78 to 96 percent fewer tokens than a full tree dump. Those numbers are self-reported. Apps that don't publish an accessibility tree are out of luck, and Electron apps can take 10 to 25 seconds to wake up. https://www.the-ai.news/en/vercel-labs-open-sources-agent-desktop/
