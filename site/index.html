Anthropic went public yesterday.

Biggest IPO ever. About $119 billion once you count the private placement.

Here's the part I keep chewing on. Nvidia, Google, Microsoft, AMD, Broadcom, TSMC all bought shares. The companies that sell the chips and the cloud are now owners of the thing that buys the chips and the cloud.

That's not a vote for one lab. That's the whole supply chain saying the same thing at the same time.

For anyone building AI for small businesses, the takeaway is pretty plain. The money has stopped arguing about whether this is real.

So the question left for the rest of us isn't "is AI going to stick?" It's "what's the boring, useful thing I can set up for one business this month?"

What's yours?
LI . NO. 02
OpenAI shipped Astra and Soul this week and the internet hated it.

One guy called them unusable. Mood on X was rough.

Then Corey Ganim ran them on real business work. An ROI calculator, a messy task, some writing. Astra actually beat Fable 5.2 on the writing.

So who's right?

Probably both. They tested different things.

A model that feels bad for a coding benchmark might be fine for writing a client email. A model everybody loves might fall apart on your weird spreadsheet.

Twenty minutes. Your own task. Your own data. That's the only review that counts.

What's the last model you judged on someone else's vibes?
LI . NO. 03
A roofing software company just shipped custom agents.

Here's what an owner can set up in plain English:

Follow up on invoices at 30, 60 and 90 days.

Flag every signed job that still has no date on the calendar.

Email the owner every Monday with the jobs that slipped.

That's it. No chatbot. No "AI transformation."

Just the follow-up work that falls apart in storm season because everybody's on a roof.

This is the shape of AI that small businesses actually pay for. A weight removal product.

What's the task in your business that only gets done when someone remembers?
