Daily AI Pulse - 2026-10-13

Today's headline

Small businesses aren't just buying AI anymore. They're building it.

Pax8 surveyed 1,150 business leaders. Of the businesses using AI, 67% have built at least one solution that's in regular use. Most of them did it with no documented review. And 49% of the ones paying for AI got a bill bigger than they expected.

So the build part is solved. The part that's wide open is who owns it after it's built.

LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
Two-thirds of small businesses using AI have built their own tools now.

Not bought. Built. Workflows, agents, custom connections to an AI provider's API.

That's from a Pax8 survey of 1,150 business leaders. In most markets they surveyed, those builds got no formal, documented review.

And 49% of the businesses paying for AI got a bill higher than they expected.

So the owner built a thing, nobody checked it, and the invoice showed up bigger than planned.

Meanwhile 55% of decision-makers spend three or more hours a week editing and fact-checking what the AI produced.

Nobody's saying stop building. Building is the good news.

But somebody has to ask "what does this touch, and what does it cost when it runs all day?"

For most small shops that person is the owner, on a Tuesday night, after close.

Who's that person at your place?
LI . NO. 02
Everyone's building AI products for each other.

Meanwhile a roofer is paying $228 a lead to send people to a page of leaks.

That's from The Boring Marketer's latest episode. There are 36.2 million small businesses in the US. Only 19.8% use AI at all, and not the way we do.

They don't want automation. They want the phone to ring.

57% say reaching customers is their biggest problem. Nobody's asking the septic guy about agents. They're asking how to get booked.

So the pitch doesn't have the word AI in it. It says more booked jobs.

The AI is how you build the funnel in days instead of weeks. The client never needs to hear that part.

His math: a $3,000 install and a $1,500 monthly retainer, against jobs worth $15,000 to $20,000.

Would a roofer pay for more booked jobs? I think a roofer would pay for more booked jobs.
LI . NO. 03
Corey Ganim laid out a 90 day plan for small business owners using Claude. One workflow a week. Admin first, then sales follow-up, then delivery.

The part I keep thinking about is weeks 3 to 7.

By week 3 you've shipped three workflows and gotten maybe 10 or 15 minutes back a week. Week 4, maybe 20.

That's where people quit. It feels like nothing's working.

His point: workflows shipped is the lead measure. Time back is the lag measure. The hours show up later, like a hockey stick.

His math lands around 15 hours a week by day 90 if you get 10 to 12 workflows live. He also says it might only be 5 for you. That's still an hour a day.

The human stays in the loop. AI drafts, you approve. Pricing and key client relationships stay yours.

The pit is the part nobody posts about.

What's one thing you do every week that shouldn't need you?

Enterprise AI moves

  • Decision models are the new race - Subquadratic, Microsoft, Perplexity Subquadratic launched SubQ Snap, built for the small calls an agent makes every turn: which file, which tool, did the test pass. It handles up to 384K tokens of text and images. Microsoft shipped Microsoft-Decision-1 for routing, classification and verification. Perplexity updated its decider model too. The idea is simple. Urbantz says most of the roughly 52,000 weekly changes on planned routes are still made one stop at a time by hand. That's the pattern to watch.
