Today's headline

Agents that keep working after you close the app went mainstream this week. OpenAI shipped Dots, Meta pushed Muse into small business, and Anthropic put Cowork on web and mobile.

Everybody's arguing about which one is best. The part worth sitting with is that none of them work until somebody writes down what the job actually is.

Today’s posts

Three for LinkedIn, three for Threads / IG. One click to copy.

LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
Three companies shipped an agent in the last few days that keeps working after you close the app.

OpenAI has Dots. Meta pushed Muse into small business, with QuickBooks, Shopify, Stripe and Slack connectors. Anthropic put Cowork on web and mobile.

Here's what stuck with me. None of them work if you can't say what the job is.

Hand an agent "help with my books" and you get mush. Hand it "check the accounts software every morning, send the first polite reminder, stop before anything big" and now you've got something.

That's the actual work. Not the tool. Writing down what's been living in the owner's head.

Most small businesses I talk to have never written it down. The follow-up rules. The "we always do it this way" stuff. It's one person's memory and a lot of vibes.

The agent just makes you finally do it.

So if you got a Dot tomorrow, what's the one job you could describe in five sentences?
LI . NO. 02
Corey Ganim made a video called "AI Automation Sucks, Actually."

His argument is that the build is getting cheap. Freelancers overseas will do for $500 what people charged five to twenty-five grand for a couple years ago. And the model companies keep building that stuff into the models.

I don't love the title. But the pricing part is hard to argue with.

If you charge for the build, you're racing the 19-year-old with an n8n account. You lose that race. Every time.

What doesn't get cheaper is knowing which job to automate. Or who checks the output. Or who picks up the phone when something breaks on a Tuesday.

That's not a build. That's a weight removal product.

The owner doesn't want a workflow. They want their afternoon back.

So what are you actually selling, the workflow or the afternoon?
LI . NO. 03
Chris Koerner emailed about 1,000 people offering five minutes of consulting for five bucks.

39% opened it. 35 people clicked. He ended up with 16 calls and $80.

On paper that's nothing. About 8 cents per recipient, from one plain text email. But he barely spent any time on it, because Claude ran Omnisend and Stripe through MCP.

Here's the part I keep thinking about. He says charging five bucks can do more harm than good. A 25 cent hot dog makes you wonder what's wrong with the hot dog.

Five dollars told people the advice was worth five dollars.

Now he wants to try $1,000 for an hour. Same kind of list.

I think about this every time a small business owner tells me they can't charge more. Cheap doesn't feel like a deal. It feels like a question.

Would someone pay for this? I think someone would pay more than you're asking.

TH . NO. 01
Google announced Gemini 4 Argon this week. Benchmarks look great. Half the internet posted "Google is so back."

Then you read the fine print. Nobody outside a small group of trusted cyber defenders can use it yet.

A model you can't open is basically a press release with good numbers.

I'll get excited when I can actually type into it.
TH . NO. 02
Aaron Fuks was a part-time receptionist at a siding company. He noticed a bigger company sent his boss hundreds of free leads a year.

So he started making those intros himself. $150 each. Now it's $6,000 for ten.

He didn't invent anything. He just noticed who was already sending who work.

What are you already noticing that somebody else would pay to have noticed for them?
TH . NO. 03
Meta says nothing publishes, sends or spends without your approval. Good.

But that makes the approval step the actual job. Someone still has to look at the offer, the audience, the budget and say yes.

The agent drafts the campaign in minutes. Knowing your customers isn't something you can hand off.

So who on your team is the approver, and do they know that's their job now?

New harnesses & tools

  • Cua Spaces - Runtime Wire A free Mac app where an agent works on a desktop you can watch and take over.
