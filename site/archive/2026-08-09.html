Today's headline

Claude Code can now text itself.

Anthropic shipped cross-session messaging on Mac and Linux. One coding session can ping another session running on the same machine, no more copy-pasting context between Terminal windows.

That's the part that made me stop scrolling. Not because it's flashy, because it's the exact problem anyone running parallel builds already has. Three sessions going, each one blind to the others, and you're the router. Now the sessions can just talk to each other.

LI . NO. 01
I used to be the router between my own coding sessions.

Three terminal windows open. Three different problems. And me, copying error messages from one and pasting them into another like some kind of human API.

Anthropic just fixed that. Claude Code sessions can message each other now. One session finds another one running on the same machine and just sends it what it needs.

Small feature. Doesn't get the headline treatment. But it's aimed at the actual bottleneck for anyone building fast. Not the model's intelligence. The coordination between the pieces you've already got running.

I build in short bursts between other jobs, so I've always got a handful of half-finished threads going at once. If the tools start managing the handoffs instead of me, that's real time back, not a demo.

What's the thing in your workflow right now where you're the router?
LI . NO. 02
The "I tried ChatGPT once and it wasn't that good" excuse just got a lot smaller.

That excuse used to hold up. Free tier had limits, the model wasn't great, people bounced after one bad answer.

OpenAI just made GPT-5.6 Luna the default for free users, and text chats are unlimited now. No cap.

That changes the conversation for anyone helping small business owners with AI. The gap used to be "they haven't tried it yet." Now more of them have, and the free version is actually decent.

So the pitch has to move. It's not "have you tried AI" anymore. It's "here's what's actually worth building on top of what you're already using."

The tool got easier to access. That doesn't make the value someone can add on top of it any less real.

Makes me wonder how many "let me show you ChatGPT" pitches just became irrelevant overnight.
LI . NO. 03
Most AI infrastructure is built to answer one question fast. Sail Research just raised $80 million betting that's the wrong shape for where this is going.

Every big provider optimizes for the same thing. Quick answer, single request, move on. Sail is building for agents that run for hours. Thousands of parallel tasks, not one prompt.

Their founder said it plain: "Sail exists to make intelligence abundant." No jargon dressing it up.

Think about what that actually changes. Right now most of us treat AI like a really smart search bar. Ask, get answer, ask again. A long-horizon agent is closer to handing off a job and checking back later.

That's a different relationship with the tool. Less "let me ask this." More "let me assign this."

Which of your weekly tasks could survive being handed off instead of asked about?
