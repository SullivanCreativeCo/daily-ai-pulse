LI . NO. 01
You don't need to build AI to make money from AI.

I watched a video today about selling "AI assessments." Here's the whole offer: get on a Zoom with a business owner. Ask them questions for an hour. Let a notetaker record it. Feed the transcript to Claude. Hand back a report.

That's it. That's the whole model.

Sounds too simple to charge $1,000 for. Except the business owner isn't going to build that report themselves. They know AI matters. They have no idea where to start. So they pay someone to ask the right questions and hand them a plan they can actually use.

The value was never the AI. It was the questions.

I think about this every time someone tells me their idea needs to get more complicated before it's worth charging for.

Would someone pay for this? I think someone would pay for this.

What's the simplest version of what you know that you've been skipping because it feels too easy to charge for?
LI . NO. 02
The best AI business doesn't touch AI.

That's the argument in a video I watched today about selling to Main Street. Roofers. Septic services. Pest control. HVAC.

None of these owners want an AI agent. They don't want to manage a dashboard or learn a new system. What they want is more jobs, more calls, less headaches.

That's the whole pitch. Not "we'll implement AI for you." Just "we'll get your phone ringing more and stop dropping leads on the floor."

Here's why it works better than chasing SaaS founders: the ticket sizes are bigger, one roof job can be $15,000, and almost nobody's pitching these guys anything real. They get spammed for SEO and ad campaigns every week. Nobody's showing up asking about the actual leak in their business.

It's not about changing the whole world. It's about changing someone's world. In this case, one plumber's, one missed call at a time.

Who's the most boring business you know that's quietly making real money?
