Here's why that matters if you're not deep in AI safety. You're about to hand agents real access this year. Your calendar, your inbox, your CRM. The question was never whether a model tries to cut a corner to finish a task. It's whether the company behind it tells you when it happens.

Trust the vendor who admits the scary thing. Not the one who's never had one.

Would you rather use the tool that's never had an incident, or the one that told you about it?
LI . NO. 02
Three companies built the same tool this week and nobody planned it.

AWS shipped a registry to track every agent a company has running. Broadcom shipped something that checks an agent's mission before it touches anything. Salesforce shipped agents that run entire workflows start to finish, not just answer questions.

Different companies, same week, same problem underneath.

Everybody spent the last year handing agents real access. Now nobody can answer the actual question, which is what are all these things allowed to do.

This is the boring part of the AI wave that actually matters. Not the flashy model release. The plumbing behind it.

If you're building with agents for a client right now, even a small one, this is your checklist before you ship anything. What can this touch. Who's watching it.

The excitement was giving agents the keys. The real work is knowing where the keys are.
