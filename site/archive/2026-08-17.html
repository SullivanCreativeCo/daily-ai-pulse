Today's headline

Wix built a full staff out of AI agents and pointed it straight at small business.

Not a chatbot bolted onto a website builder. A separate product called Symphony. One orchestrator agent (they named it Maestro) runs the show, and a second layer of agents checks the first layer's work before the owner ever sees it.

That checking layer is the part to sit with. Every "AI employee" pitch skips the part where somebody has to catch the AI's mistakes. Wix built that in from day one, at platform scale, for retail and consulting shops that never had staff to check anything either.

LI . NO. 02
Alibaba shipped a model that runs on a MacBook and still beats Meta's competing local model on the benchmarks they compared head to head.

That sentence would've sounded made up eighteen months ago.

"Runs locally" used to mean "the cheap, worse option." Now it means "runs locally, and it's not obviously worse." Qwen3.8-27B does coding and long-horizon agent tasks at a level that used to require a model ten times its size.

Here's why that actually matters if you build anything: the math on what a custom AI project should cost just changed. You don't need a cloud API bill scaling with every user for a lot of use cases anymore. You need a laptop.

I'm not saying rip out your API calls tomorrow. I'm saying the next client who asks "why does this need to be so expensive" deserves an honest answer, and the honest answer changed this month.

Married to one architecture is a great way to quote yourself out of a deal.
LI . NO. 03
Google put an expiration date on their own price cut.

Gemini 3.7 Flash launched cheap. $0.75 per million input tokens. That rate doubles on January 1, 2027, and they printed the date in the announcement instead of quietly raising it later like everyone else does.

I actually respect that. Most price changes in this industry happen the week you're not paying attention.

But here's the part that should change how you write contracts. If you quoted a client based on today's API pricing, and that pricing has a printed sunset date, you now have proof it's going to move. Put a clause in. "Pricing reflects vendor rates as of [date], subject to change if the underlying model provider adjusts theirs."

Nobody wants to be the one eating a rate hike on a fixed-price contract six months from now because the receipt was sitting right there and nobody read it.

What's in your contracts right now that assumes today's prices hold?
