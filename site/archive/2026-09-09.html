LI . NO. 02
Every AI pitch to a small business owner eventually hits the same wall.

Where does my data go? Who else sees it? What happens if that company gets bought or shuts down?

Mistral just raised three billion euros answering that exact question, at a valuation over 21 billion. Their pitch isn't the smartest model. It's sovereignty. Your data stays inside your walls, on infrastructure you control.

There's a smaller version of the same idea any of us can use today. Local AI. A model that runs on your own machine instead of someone else's cloud.

Listened to a breakdown this week that made it click for me: local does the private first pass on anything sensitive, cloud handles the heavy reasoning after, and a human checks anything that matters. Not either/or. Both, in the right order.

For a business owner who's nervous about AI touching customer records or financials, that's the whole pitch. Their stuff never leaves the building until they say so.

Would someone pay for that peace of mind? I think someone would pay for that peace of mind.
LI . NO. 03
A new tool launched this week that does something small and kind of important.

Fimo takes code written by Claude or Codex and keeps working on it after you close the laptop. Fixing SEO. Refreshing pages that went stale. Translating content into other languages overnight.

Here's the part that actually matters. Everybody's solved "write the code fast." Almost nobody's solved "keep it alive once it's live."

I see this constantly. Somebody builds a beautiful site or app with AI, launches it, and then it just sits there. No updates. No one watching the metrics. No one asking why traffic dropped in March.

The building part got easy. The tending part is still a job. A real one, that somebody has to actually do.

That's not a knock on the tools. It's the opportunity sitting right next to them.

If you can build fast now, what are you doing with the time you used to spend building?
