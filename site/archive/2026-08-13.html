LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
"Managing agents" is already the wrong phrase.

I heard this on a podcast this week and it stopped me mid-drive.

Alli Miller runs a fleet of 34 AI agents. She used to think about it like being their manager. Susie go here, Betty go there. Now she says that's wrong. She's three rungs up. Setting up the infrastructure, then waiting for the escalations.

Her best prompt to the whole workforce is three words. "Do smart things."

That's not a hack. That's a mindset shift. You stop being the person who assigns tasks and become the person who decides what actually matters.

I think about this with clients too. Nobody wants a tool. They want someone who decides what should happen with the tool.

What are you still micromanaging that you could just trust to run?
LI . NO. 02
Claude is now hiding a watermark inside every word it writes.

Not images. Text. Anthropic rolled this out quietly and it's already in your code, your emails, your client deliverables if you're using Claude.

It's invisible. It's not in the metadata. It's baked into the actual word choices the model makes, so it travels when you copy and paste something out of it.

A developer named Nick Dobos put it plainly. "Claude adding invisible watermarks inside my code base? Total BS."

Here's what got me. A watermark on a photo makes sense, deepfakes are a real problem. But a watermark on a legal quote, or a line of production code, means the model is quietly shaping its own word choices to leave a signature.

I don't think this breaks anything today. I think it's worth knowing it's there before you find out the hard way.

What would you actually do differently if you knew every output had an invisible tag on it?
LI . NO. 03
Free AI isn't generosity. It's bait, and that's fine.

OpenAI just made ChatGPT unlimited for free users. New model, a real reasoning button, no caps.

The internet's first reaction was "how is that even profitable?"

It's profitable because free was never the product. Free is the hook. Get enough people living inside your app every day and some percentage upgrades, some percentage clicks an ad, and the whole thing pays for itself on volume.

Small businesses do this too, they just don't call it that. A free consult. A free lead magnet. A free first project.

The mistake is thinking free means giving away the thing you'd normally charge for. It doesn't. Free means removing the friction of trying you at all.

Would someone pay for what you're giving away for free? If the answer's no, you're not running a funnel. You're just doing free work.

Whatever you think about the messenger, the question underneath it is a good one. Who ends up holding the power once this stuff gets good enough to actually matter?

Most of the AI conversation right now is doom on one side and hype on the other. Not a lot of people asking who actually benefits when the dust settles.

I don't know the answer yet. But I know which side of that question I want to be building on.
