LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
There's a public leaderboard that tells you exactly how much money software resellers make.

It's HighLevel's awards page. Gold, platinum, diamond, based on customer count. Customer count is public. Price is public. That's napkin math for revenue, sitting out in the open.

I watched Chris Koerner walk through it. A guy selling missed-call-text-back software to pest control companies is clearing 30k a month. No app built from scratch. No original code. Same software as a hundred other resellers, different logo.

The part that actually matters isn't the software. It's the niche. He picked roofers, or dentists, or bounce house rental companies, and went deep instead of wide.

Would someone pay for a tool that just answers the phone when they can't? I think someone would pay for that. A lot of someones already are.

Your ideas aren't bad ideas. They're just not finished ideas. Sometimes finished just means you picked a lane.
LI . NO. 02
Three different AI companies shipped harness news this week. DeepSeek open sourced one. WRITER upgraded theirs. Another project tried to build one universal API for all of them.

Nobody's arguing about harnesses on LinkedIn yet. Give it a month.

Here's the plain version. The model is the brain. The harness is what lets the brain actually do something, remember what happened five steps ago, call the right tool, touch a real file, report back when it's done.

For a while everyone obsessed over which model was smartest. That race is basically a tie now. The next fight is who builds the scaffolding around the model that makes it useful inside an actual business.

I've felt this firsthand building tools day to day. The model was never the hard part. Getting it to reliably do the boring, repeatable stuff, that's the whole game.

If it's repeatable, it's a skill. Turns out that's true for agents too.
LI . NO. 03
Zhipu released GLM-5.3 this week. Free weights coming in two weeks. It's already being credited with finding a real vulnerability in Cursor, the coding tool SpaceX just bought.

Same week, Qwen dropped a model with a million tokens of context under an Apache license. No cost. No login wall.

I used to think open weight models were the consolation prize. The thing you used because you couldn't afford the good one.

That's not true anymore. These are the tools actual companies are shipping products on right now.

The move here isn't picking a favorite model. It's staying loose enough to swap the model out the second a better free one lands, because that's happening monthly now, not yearly.

Married to an idea is a great way to get forgotten. Turns out being married to a model does the same thing.
