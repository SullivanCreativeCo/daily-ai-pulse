LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
Meta's AI agent fixed a tracking problem that a human and a support team couldn't fix in months.

Chris Koerner told this story. His newsletter ads on Facebook said one thing. Beehive said another. Nobody could tell which ad brought which subscriber.

So he handed the whole mess to an agent.

Here's the part I keep thinking about. It didn't just find the problem. It subscribed to his newsletter through the ad link, like a fake customer, and watched the data land on the other side.

It tested its own fix.

That's the whole difference. Most of us use AI to write a thing and then hope the thing works. This one checked.

Whenever I hand something to an agent now, I ask one question first. How will you know it worked?

If it can't answer that, I don't trust the output yet.

What's one task you handed off where you never actually checked the result?
LI . NO. 02
Google shipped Gemini 4 Hydrogen yesterday. The model is fine. The pairing is the story.

Carbon and Argon are the big thinkers. Hydrogen is the fast one that writes the code, runs the tests, checks the work.

Inside Antigravity there's now an Orchestrator that swaps between them in the same session. Google says it got near-Carbon results while using 24% less quota and finishing 19% faster.

The price tells the same story. Hydrogen is $1.50 per million input tokens, flat across a million-token window. One tester ran Carbon through a bug hunt for $31. Another frontier model cost almost $140 on the same run.

Here's why that matters if you run a small business. You don't need the smartest model for every step.

You need a smart one to plan and a cheap one to do the reps.

Do more with the same, not the same with less.

Which step in your workflow is a Carbon doing Hydrogen's job?
LI . NO. 03
A new open-source project called AgentRun has a simple idea I like a lot.

A smart model does the job the first time. Tools, instructions, the works. At the end it writes a little retro. What I did, what I'd do better.

While it works, it also writes a workflow for the parts it knows it can do the same way every time.

The team says that after about 100 cases, the agent mostly just runs the workflow start to finish instead of thinking its way through. Workflows are cheap. So the job gets cheaper the more you repeat it.

If it's repeatable, it's a skill.

That's true for people too. The first invoice follow-up takes thought. The fiftieth shouldn't.

Most small businesses have a dozen of those. Quotes. Intake. Chasing a late payment. Nobody wrote them down, so nobody could hand them off.

Would someone pay for a version of that that just runs? I think someone would.

What's the thing you do every week that you could write the steps for tonight?
