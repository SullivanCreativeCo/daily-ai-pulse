LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
Anthropic made computer use, skills, and the files API official this week.

Same week, Microsoft published a study on the same class of tools. Best model in the test completed a task correctly on the first try about 65% of the time.

Ask it to do that same task right 20 times in a row, and it dropped to 25%.

So agents are officially production ready. And they're still flaky under repetition. Both of those are true right now, at the same time.

Here's the part nobody's saying loud enough: "it worked once" and "it works" are not the same claim. If you're building anything on agents right now, the second one is the only one that matters.

How many times have you actually tested the same task before trusting it?
LI . NO. 02
Z.ai has always been the lab that just ships open weights. No drama, no delay.

This week they broke that pattern. GLM-5.3 launched, you can use it through their API today, but the download page says "Coming Soon."

The reason is the actual story. The model got good at offensive security faster than they expected. So they're holding the weights back for a safety review before anyone can download and run it themselves.

That's a different kind of announcement than "we shipped a new model." That's a lab admitting the thing got sharper than the plan accounted for.

Most people will read the headline and move on. The interesting question is what happens in two weeks when that review is done, and what they decide is safe enough to release.
LI . NO. 03
For two years, "open weights" in AI has come with an asterisk. Good, but not that good. Open, but a step behind.

Kimi K3 just tied for first on one of the main intelligence benchmarks. Fully open weight. No asterisk.

That matters more than the score itself. It means the gap between "open source" and "frontier" just got a lot smaller, which changes who gets to build on top of the best tools without a subscription gating them out.

Most people chase brand names in AI. Which model is the popular one, which one's trending on their feed. The better move has always been picking what actually does the job.

Would someone pay for the output either way? That's the only question worth asking.
