LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
Four months ago a college kid was on a treadmill listening to a podcast.

Now he and two friends have a $30 million construction company paying them $15,000 a month.

No office. No employees. No connections.

Here's what they did. They texted everyone in their phones. Not a pitch. They asked for advice on the business model. Who do you know that owns a company this size and needs help with AI?

Hundreds of texts. That was their number one source of leads.

They'd tried walking into small businesses first. Problems everywhere, no budget. So they moved up to bigger fish and let their network do the finding.

Here's what gets me. Everybody's hunting for a clever channel. The channel was the contacts app.

It's awkward, sure. Nobody wants to sell to their friends. But if you love the people you know, solving their problems isn't selling. It's just help with a price on it.

Who in your phone owns a business that's drowning in busywork?
LI . NO. 02
OpenAI shipped GPT-6.1 Terra today. The part I care about isn't the benchmark.

It's the price. Half of what the last Terra cost.

Their own example is reading incoming invoices and typing the details into accounting software.

That's the most boring job in the building. And it's exactly the kind of job a small business owner never automated, because the math never worked. Too much volume, too little margin, too many hours.

A cheaper model changes the math.

A bookkeeper working through a couple hundred invoices a month isn't getting replaced. She stops typing and starts checking. Different job. Better job.

That's the whole play for Main Street. Do more with the same, not the same with less.

One caveat. These are OpenAI's own numbers. I'd test it on one real stack of invoices before telling a client anything.

What's the most boring task in your business that was never worth automating until now?
LI . NO. 03
Most AI website builders have a bad habit.

They fill the blanks. No reviews yet? Here's a rating. No years in business? Here's a number. The owner finds out when a customer brings it up.

A builder called TruLata launched today and it does the opposite. Every sentence has to trace back to something the owner said, their current site, or their Google Business Profile. Reviews show up word for word. Claims about guarantees or awards wait for a human reviewer.

If you ask for a rating you haven't shown, it asks where it came from.

I haven't built a site with it. This is from the launch announcement, so take it as that.

But the idea is right. For a small business the website is basically the handshake. An invented handshake gets expensive.

Simple rule. Don't write what you can't point to.

Where's your line on what AI is allowed to make up?
