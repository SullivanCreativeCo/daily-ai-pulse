Today's headline

Everybody shipped an agent that logs into your tools and does the work. Grok Bot, ChatGPT agents in Slack, Cohere North 2, FPT's digital worker.

The part worth sitting with is that every one of them has an approval step. The Grok Bot demo drafts 36 emails overnight and the user approves 10. That review gate is the product, more than the autonomy is.

LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
Every agent launch this week hides the same feature.

xAI shipped Grok Bot. It logs into your tools and does the work. In the demo it drafts 36 outbound emails overnight. The user approves 10. The rest wait.

FPT showed a digital worker that preps quotes and contracts. A human still signs. Cohere's new platform has autonomy policies that cap what an agent can do without sign-off.

Three companies. Same move.

So the selling point isn't how much the agent can do alone. It's that it stops and asks.

Think about the owner of a 12-person shop. They will never hand over the send button. But they'd pay to wake up to 36 drafts and a yes or no.

That's not a chatbot. That's a weight removal product.

Would someone pay for an agent that does 90 percent of the work and leaves the last click to them? I think someone would.

What's the last click in your week that you'd never hand off?
LI . NO. 02
Two numbers from this week stuck with me.

$472 and $4.87.

Anchor ran 300 web tasks two ways. A frontier model did 93 percent of them for $472. A small model with their new Action Index did 93.3 percent for $4.87.

The trick is boring. It remembers the workflows that already worked and replays them as code. The model only steps in when a page changes.

This is the reason I care. The first time you do a task, it's expensive. The hundredth time should be nearly free.

If it's repeatable, it's a skill. And a skill that gets cheaper every run is something you can build a business around.

If you're doing automation work for local businesses, that changes how you think about pricing. You're not selling the model. You're selling the memory of what already worked.

What's a task you've explained to a tool more than three times?
LI . NO. 03
Three giant open models dropped in a few days.

Meta's Muse Spark Open is 816B parameters. Reflection's Beam is 501B. Microsoft announced a 2T one.

Here's the honest part. Reflection suggests four B200 GPUs to run Beam well. Microsoft says its model needs your own fine-tuning before it does anything useful.

So no, your client with the HVAC company isn't running these.

But here's why I still watch. Every time a lab gives away something this big, the price of the models people rent has to move. Google's new Gemini 4 Argon launches at $2 per million input tokens, and that doubles after November 30.

The cost of the thinking keeps dropping. The part that doesn't drop is knowing which task to hand it.

That part is still yours.

Which task in your business would you hand to a model if it cost almost nothing?
