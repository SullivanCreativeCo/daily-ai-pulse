LI . NO. 02
Every agent story this week has the same second sentence.

First sentence is always the capability. Look what it can do now.

Second sentence is always the leash. And it asks for approval before it acts.

OpenAI's new workspace agents draft the email, then wait. Amazon's agents can pay for things on their own now, but only inside guardrails somebody set up first. Two separate companies built entire products this week whose only job is catching the moment an agent needs a human and routing it fast.

Nobody's really selling raw capability anymore. That part got solved.

What's actually for sale right now is the pause button. The part that lets a business owner say yes before something happens instead of finding out after.

That's not a small feature. That's the whole reason anyone lets an agent near their business in the first place.

The next wave of AI tools won't win on power. They'll win on how easy the "no" is.
LI . NO. 03
A company this week banned lazy AI writing. Not AI writing.

Clay's co-founder posted their internal policy and it spread everywhere. Not "don't use AI." The rule was simpler. Stand behind every sentence you publish. Writing is thinking, so if you skip the process you walk away dumber about your own topic. And longer isn't better. If a doc came from a two line prompt, just share the two lines.

That last one got me. How many decks and posts and docs out there are just a prompt wearing a costume.

I still write my own hooks. Not because AI can't draft one. Because the fifteen minutes I spend fighting a sentence is the fifteen minutes I actually understand what I'm trying to say.

AI can write the copy. It can't do the thinking for you, even when it looks like it did.

Have you actually read the last thing you posted, or just the prompt that made it?
