Today's headline

Three different companies shipped the same idea today, and none of them planned it together.

Slack launched Code, so you can hand a coding task to an agent right inside a channel. NanoClaw showed up in Slack too, letting you spin up a whole team of agents from one message. Zephyr took its own version of this live as well.

Same pitch, three times, same week: put the agent in the room with your team instead of locked in a private chat tab.

That's not a coincidence. That's the industry deciding the chatbot window was step one, not the finish line.

That's the tell. When three separate companies solve the same problem the same week, it's not a coincidence, it's a signal.

The chatbot window was never the end state. It was the beginning. The real shift is agents becoming teammates you manage instead of tools you prompt.

If you run a team right now, ask yourself something. Are you still treating AI like a search bar? Or are you starting to treat it like staff?
LI . NO. 02
A CRM just launched built for businesses with fewer than five people.

BlueFaucet is going after solopreneurs. Home services, personal care, food and beverage. The owner who's too busy running the shop to ever follow up with a customer.

HoneyBook did something similar this week. They hooked their whole system into Claude so small business owners can actually ask questions of their own data instead of digging through it.

Here's the part that stood out to me. McKinsey found that almost half of companies over five billion in revenue have already moved AI past the pilot stage. Companies under a hundred million? Twenty nine percent.

That gap has been real for a while.

What's new is companies finally building for the small end instead of trickling tools down from enterprise years later.

Would a solo shop owner pay for something that runs their follow ups while they're on a job? I think they would.
LI . NO. 03
Z.ai didn't train a new model this week. They just got better at teaching the one they had.

GLM-5.3 uses the exact same base as the last version. Same size, same architecture. Every gain came from post-training. And the jump was big enough that one benchmark went from under five percent to over twenty eight.

They're delaying the open weight release two weeks to run more safety checks, because the same training run that made it better at coding also made it noticeably better at offensive security work. Nobody planned that part.

The lesson isn't really about this one model. It's about what "better" means right now.

For a while the story was bigger model, bigger gains. This week the story was the same model, better teaching, bigger gains anyway.

If it's repeatable, it's a skill. Apparently that's true for models too.
