LI . NO. 01
One in eight small businesses is using AI now.

In 2023 it was one in 34.

That's from the CEO of Novo, who works with over 250,000 small businesses. So it's his customer base, not the whole country. Worth knowing before you quote it.

The direction is the point.

Look at where he says it's landing. Trucking. Restaurants. Not the flashy stuff. The back office. Invoices, scheduling, payroll.

AI can't drive the truck. It can rewire everything behind the truck.

And seven in eight haven't started.

That's not a tools gap. The tools are there. It's a gap in somebody sitting down with a business owner and asking what eats their Tuesday.

Would someone pay to get that Tuesday back? I think someone would.

What's the dullest task in your business that you'd hand off first?
LI . NO. 02
Microsoft says its new coding model gets 96% of Claude Opus 5.5's performance at 5% of the price.

Their benchmarks. Their promo pricing. Take it with salt.

But even if it's half true, look at what's happening.

Good enough is getting cheap. Fast.

A year ago the question was whether the model could do it. Now the question is what you build when the model costs almost nothing.

That changes who wins. When the model is the expensive part, the big shops win. When the model is the cheap part, the person who understands the customer's actual problem wins.

The plumber's scheduling headache. The dentist's no-show problem. Those don't need the best model on earth. They need somebody who gets the mess.

Do more with the same, not the same with less.

What would you build if the model cost basically nothing?
LI . NO. 03
Google's new agent sandbox does one thing I really like.

The agent never holds the keys.

It runs in an isolated box. When it needs to call something, a gateway outside the box adds the credentials. The agent asks. The gateway opens the door. The key stays on the other side.

It's not shipped yet. Preview is "the coming months," and there's no pricing. So this is a design, not a product.

But the design is the lesson.

Most of us hand an agent a key and hope. API key in an env file, agent can read it, fingers crossed.

An agent will make mistakes. That's the job description. The real question is what it can reach when it does.

If your whole setup depends on the agent behaving, you don't have a setup. You have a hope.

Who's holding the keys in your agent workflows?

THREADS / IG CAPTIONS

TH . NO. 01
Google shipped an API that doesn't write anything.

You ask it a yes or no, pick one, or score this. It hands back an answer and how sure it is. No paragraph to parse.

Honestly that's most small business AI work. Is this email a quote request? Is this lead worth a call?

Nobody needs an essay for that. Most of the useful stuff is just a yes or a no.
TH . NO. 02
Gemini can now hold a huge pile of tools and only load the one it needs.

Before, every tool description rode along on every single request. A few dozen functions can eat tens of thousands of tokens. Like carrying your whole garage to the grocery store.

But Google's own docs say if you have under 20 tools, just load them all.

So don't overbuild it.

New harnesses & tools

  • Tool Retrieval in the Gemini API (preview) - MarkTechPost The model sees only a tool's name and short description, and the full schema loads when it's needed. A few dozen function declarations can eat tens of thousands of tokens on every request. Google's docs say to just load everything if you have under 20 stable tools. It pays off once you wire up a lot of MCP servers. https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/22/google-gemini-api-tool-retrieval/

  • Google Agent Compute Engine - InfoQ Agents run in isolated microVMs that can pause and resume. The credentials never sit inside the sandbox, because an outside gateway adds them to each request. It isn't available yet. No pricing, and preview is "the coming months." Treat it as a design, but the credentials-outside idea is worth stealing. https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/google-agent-compute-engine/

  • Cognition's Devin Bots - The Next Gen Tech Insider A coordinator splits a big engineering goal into subtasks and sends multiple Devin agents at them in parallel. Beta testers reported 4.5x more merged pull requests. That figure comes from Cognition's product page.
