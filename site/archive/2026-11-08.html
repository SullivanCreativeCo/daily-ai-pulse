Today's headline

Open-weight models got seriously good this week, and two of the biggest dropped within days of each other.

Moonshot released the full weights for Kimi K3.1 Pro. Meta released Muse Spark Open. Near-frontier smart is getting cheap to rent, and that changes what's worth building on top of it.

LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
78% of small business owners say AI saves them time every week.

43% of those same owners say their revenue hasn't moved.

That's from a Bluehost study of 350 U.S. owners, passed along in a Fortune piece this week. Small sample, so don't tattoo it on anything. But it matches what I keep seeing.

Saving an hour isn't the win. The hour is just sitting there afterward.

Most folks pour it back into volume. More emails. More posts. One owner's automation sent about 400,000 emails in three days. His line: "I don't have 400,000 customers."

The better move is boring. Take the hour and put it on the calendar against the ten people most likely to send you work.

Do more with the same, not the same with less.

Where does your saved hour actually go on a Tuesday?
LI . NO. 02
Most bad builds don't fail at the code.

They fail at the first assumption nobody questioned.

Google open-sourced a plugin this week called Elephant-Goldfish. The idea is simple. One long session holds all the context and writes a spec. No code yet. Then fresh agents with zero history read that spec and try to tear it apart.

Zero history is the whole trick. The one who wrote it can't see the hole. The one who just walked in can.

Their pitch is catching wrong assumptions in a Markdown file instead of in a pull request. Cheap place to be wrong.

Same move works on business ideas. Your ideas aren't bad ideas. They're just not finished ideas. Somebody who wasn't inside your head for three weeks will find the unfinished part in ten minutes.

Who's your goldfish?
LI . NO. 03
Moonshot put out the weights for Kimi K3.1 Pro this week. 2.8 trillion parameters. On one agentic coding test, their own numbers have it within a point of Claude Opus 5.5.

Their own numbers, so grain of salt. Artificial Analysis has it three points back on its index, which is still the closest an open model has gotten.

The download is about 1.56 terabytes. Nobody's running that on a laptop.

So who cares? The providers who can serve it. Any of them with the hardware can host it now, and prices follow. Meta put out Muse Spark Open too, with hosted options live on day one.

Here's what I take from that. Near-frontier smart is getting cheap to rent. The model stops being the product.

What's left is knowing which annoying, repeatable job a business owner would happily hand off.

Would someone pay for that? I think someone would pay for that.

No accuracy numbers. No time-saved numbers. Hacker News was skeptical.

I'm not saying don't try it. I'm saying ask one question first. What's the worst thing it could do with email access?

If you can live with that answer, give it one recurring job and watch it for a week.

A guy in La Crosse launched an AI setup business this week. He installs a lead assistant plus a few specialist workers on a small business's own computer.

No account managers. No tickets.

Everybody's selling the agents. What owners seem to want is someone to call when the agent does something weird.

  • Kimi K3.1 Pro weights are public - AI Frontier Post Moonshot published the weights on Nov 4. It's 2.8T parameters, and Moonshot's own tests put it within a point of Claude Opus 5.5 on agentic coding (65.7 vs 66.4). Weights are on Hugging Face and hosted APIs were live on day one (Together, Fireworks, Groq, OpenRouter, and others). The license wants "Built with Muse Spark Open" attribution. Big hosting providers over $20M revenue need a separate deal. Meta calls it the most capable US open-weight model, and 9to6AI hasn't tested it hands-on yet. https://9to6ai.com/muse-spark-open/

  • Baidu ships ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base, with no post-training - AI In Context 280B total and 14B active, open on Hugging Face. It's raw on purpose, so you shape it yourself. New harnesses & tools

  • Elephant-Goldfish Model plugin - MarkTechPost Google open-sourced it. One long session drafts a spec and writes no code. Then fresh subagents with zero history try to tear the spec apart before any code gets written. It works with Claude Code 2.1.271+, Codex, and Antigravity. There's no accuracy benchmark, and one open issue shows instruction files leaking into reviewers on Codex. A builder would care because it catches wrong assumptions in a Markdown file instead of in a pull request. https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/08/google-elephant-goldfish-model-plugin-spec-review-coding-agents/

  • Framer Harness 2 - Tech Bytes The agent now writes JavaScript that executes while it streams, instead of emitting one command at a time. Framer reports up to 33% cheaper, 20% faster, and half the tool steps. If an early check fails, it cancels the stream and stops paying for tokens. That "check first, bail early" move is worth stealing for your own agents. https://techbytes.app/posts/harness-2-executing-agent-code-as-it-streams/

  • Google ADK 2.14.0 - AI Weekly A/A2A and execution environments graduate from experimental, and there's a new TeamsRunner for Microsoft Teams. There are three breaking changes, including RunSkillScriptTool no longer taking a free-form command. If you build on ADK, read the upgrade notes first. It's worth it mainly if your clients live in Teams. https://aiweekly.co/alerts/google-adk-2140-graduates-core-modules-adds-teams-runner
