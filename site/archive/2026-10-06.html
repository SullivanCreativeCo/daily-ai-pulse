LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
Two of the biggest AI companies pointed their agents at small business in the same week.

Meta shipped Muse for Small Business. OpenAI shipped Dots. Both work in the background, both plug into the tools you already pay for, both come back when they need a yes.

The tech isn't the hard part anymore. Connecting Shopify and Stripe and QuickBooks takes about ten minutes.

The hard part is deciding what the agent gets to touch.

The smartest advice I saw this week was the boring kind. Start read-only. One task. Let it summarize your week across ads, sales and Instagram before it sends anything or spends anything.

That's low risk, and you find out fast whether it's any good.

Hand over every login on day one because the demo looked slick, and one wrong email later the whole thing gets unplugged.

Earn the trust in small steps. Same way you would with a new hire.

LI . NO. 02
Can the agent do it and should the agent do it are two different questions.

Logistics Reply just put out a framework for warehouse AI that I think applies way past warehouses. Five levels of authority. Inform. Recommend. Act. Coordinate. Governed autonomy.

Nobody starts at the top.

An agent that tells you a dock door is double booked is Inform. One that suggests a new slot is Recommend. One that rebooks it on its own is Act. Same agent. Very different amount of trust.

Most owners jump straight to the scary question. Will it replace someone?

The better one is what level does this task get today?

Invoice reminders? Probably Act. Refunds over a hundred bucks? Recommend, and a human clicks the button.

Then you move it up when the evidence says to. Not when the vendor says to.

You're not deciding whether to trust AI. You're deciding how much, for which job, this month.

What's one task you'd move up a level right now?
LI . NO. 03
Not every decision needs a genius.

AWS just open-sourced a model called Strands Decider 2B. About 1.9 billion parameters. It doesn't write anything. It picks between options you give it and tells you how confident it is.

That's it. Route A or route B, plus a confidence score.

Think about how much of your automation is exactly that. Is this lead qualified? Does this email need a human? Is this invoice a duplicate?

A lot of us have been paying frontier model prices for questions a much smaller model can answer.

OpenAI is testing a similar Decisions API, so the idea is showing up from both sides. Big model for the thinking, small model for the sorting.

If you build for small businesses, that matters. Cheaper per decision means more workflows that actually pencil out. Nobody pays a monthly fee for something that costs more to run than the problem it solves.
