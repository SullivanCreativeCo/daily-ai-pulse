LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
Everybody picks the cool category. That's why the cool category is full.

Chris Koerner dropped a video today where he hands an AI agent six rules and tells it to go find a boring corner of the internet with a mess in it.

The rules are plain. The buyer just had something happen to them. The product stops a loss bigger than its price. Nobody has organized the category. The same item has a wild price spread. Demand shows up on a calendar. And it's boring.

His example was fruit trees. Same tree, $30 on one site, $180 on another. Nobody competing, so the prices never squeezed together.

Here's what stuck with me. Boring is the filter. If it sounds like a gadget, somebody already made a video about it.

Small businesses are full of boring. Scheduling. Paperwork. Follow-ups nobody sends.

What are you already doing for yourself that someone else would find useful?
LI . NO. 02
Zoho just put a vibe coding builder inside its CRM.

Describe a dashboard in plain words. It builds it from your live CRM data. No export, no third-party tool, no deploy.

That last part is the whole story.

The scary thing about vibe coding for a business owner was never the coding. It was shipping customer data to some other platform so a tool could read it.

Zoho's pitch is that you don't move the data. The app lives where the data already lives. There's even a security review step before you publish.

I haven't used it, so I can't tell you how good the output is.

But the direction is right. The tool shows up where the work already is, not the other way around.

Do more with the same, not the same with less.

Think about the little tracker that never made it onto anyone's roadmap. That's usually where this kind of thing earns its keep.

Which one would you build first?
LI . NO. 03
Overcut ran fifteen tickets through parallel AI agents and published the numbers.

18.2 hours of workflow time fit into 9.7 active hours. Sounds like the headline.

It's not. Here's the line I kept re-reading.

The full run took 22.5 hours. For 12.8 of those, nothing was running. And 12.4 of those hours were the system waiting on a person to answer a gate. One stretch was nine hours overnight.

So once the agents run side by side, the slowest part of the whole project is somebody replying.

They kept the gates, by the way. Plan approval and high-risk landings still get a human. Good call.

But the question gets sharper now. Which decisions really need a person, and which ones are sitting there because that's how we always did it?

I think most of us have never sorted those two piles.

Where would you start?

AI launches & news

  • Perplexity and Intelligence - OpenAI Jakub Pachocki shows how their models' loss on a held-out dataset has moved. GPT-3.5 sat around 0.99 and GPT-4 around 0.91. The newer models are under 0.8. He expects another window of surprises around 0.68 to 0.7. The ask is simple. Labs agree on a shared metric and a first limit they won't cross until safety monitoring catches up. He's honest about the weak spots. Different labs use different datasets, and chain-of-thought doesn't show up in this number at all. https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence/

  • GLM-5.4 - Z.ai They got a bigger-feeling model without adding parameters. They took 28 layers of their base model and ran them twice. They call it a Loop Transformer. They say it lands in 2T to 3T parameter territory. They didn't publish benchmark scores for GLM-5.4 itself, so hold that claim loosely. It's out now for Coding Plan subscribers. API and open weights come after safety checks. Their own listed weakness is "marathon thinking" loops. https://z.ai/blog/glm-5.4

  • Kling Image 4.0 - Kling AI Native 8K, up to 2,000 characters of text in one image, and up to 10 matching images from one prompt. That last one is the useful bit for anyone making product sets or storyboards. Text and images edit on separate layers. https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/23/3408138/0/en/kling-ai-launches-kling-image-4-0-with-photographic-realism-and-native-8k-generation.html

  • GPT-6 Cyber - OpenAI A security-tuned model for vetted defenders. OpenAI says it scored 48.5% on ExploitGym, up from 32.1% for the last one. It found 81 previously unknown high-severity Firefox bugs. Access is limited to approved customers for now. The page I was handed was in Marathi, so I'm going off the numbers only. https://openai.com/mr-IN/index/introducing-gpt-6-cyber/
