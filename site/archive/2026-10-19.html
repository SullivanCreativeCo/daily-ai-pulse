LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
Nine out of ten small businesses are using or testing AI. Fewer than one in four wrote down a rule for it.

That's from Pax8's latest SMB survey. Pax8 sells to these folks, so take it with a grain of salt. But the gap feels right.

Nobody sat down and decided to use AI. Somebody pasted a customer email into ChatGPT on a Tuesday. Then another person did. Now it's just how the place runs.

And nearly half of them found tools in the business they never approved. In six months. That's all it took.

Here's why that matters. The owner isn't asking for a platform. They're asking two things. Is this safe? What's it costing me?

Right now nobody's answering either one.

So the work isn't building another bot. It's walking in, finding out what's already running, writing one page of rules, and putting a cap on the bill.

That's not just a service, that's a weight removal product.

Who in a small business is actually supposed to own this?
LI . NO. 02
Automating a bad process just gives you a faster bad process.

Irene Becker wrote that this week, and she learned it running 99aupairs.

Here's what I like about her story. She didn't start with the AI. She started with a problem.

Candidates apply from Europe while her team in Australia is asleep. Onboarding used to take three to seven days. Forms, interviews, follow-ups, everybody hunting for a time that works.

So they built an interview flow where candidates answer one question at a time, whenever they want. Now it takes under 24 hours. Someone can apply overnight and be ready to meet a family by the time the team logs on.

Nobody lost a job. The waiting just went away.

Most people start with "what can AI do?" That's the wrong end of it. Start with "where is somebody waiting?"

Then ask if AI is even the answer. Sometimes the process is just broken.

Where's the waiting in your business?
LI . NO. 03
A new tool called MemoryLane watches your screen, writes a short note on what you're doing, and deletes the screenshot. After a few days it ranks your repetitive work by hours saved.

$100 a month for one person. The company says the notes stay on your laptop unless you choose to share them. That's their claim, and I haven't tried it.

But the idea is the part I care about.

Most owners can't tell you what's repetitive. They're too deep in it. The invoice reminder, the copy-paste from email into the CRM, the same three questions answered every week. It all just feels like work.

If it's repeatable, it's a skill. And if it's repeatable, somebody can probably automate it.

The hard part was never building the automation. It was knowing which task deserved one.

So here's a fun exercise. Guess how many hours a week you spend on stuff you could write down as steps.

Then go find out how wrong you were.
