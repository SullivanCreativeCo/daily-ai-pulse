LI . NO. 01
Notion just shipped a feature I've been doing by hand for six months.

I keep a folder of instructions. One file per repeatable task. Voice rules, contract templates, how I want a digest formatted.

Notion just built that into the product. They're calling it Agent Skills. Write the instructions once, save it as a file, any agent can run it.

Here's the part that actually matters. Those files export as SKILL.md. Same format Claude Code uses. Same format Cursor and Codex can read.

That means the skill isn't stuck inside one app anymore. Write it once, use it everywhere.

If it's repeatable, it's a skill. I've said that for a while. Feels less like a saying now and more like where every tool is headed.

What's the one thing you keep re-explaining to a chatbot that you could just write down once and be done with?
LI . NO. 02
Two companies shipped AI models this week built to never leave your machine.

Meta released one. Liquid AI released another, small enough to run on a Raspberry Pi.

Why does that matter more than the size number? Because right now, every time you type something into ChatGPT, it's leaving your building.

For a small business owner that's client names, pricing, half-finished proposals. All of it going somewhere you don't control.

A model that runs locally changes that math. Your code, your customer notes, your pricing sheet, they stay on the machine. No server round trip.

Would someone pay for that? I think someone would pay for that. Especially the business owner who's been quietly nervous about where their data actually goes.

The AI conversation has been about "how smart" for two years. This week a few labs started answering "how private" instead.
LI . NO. 03
Anthropic released two models this week. You can only use one of them.

Fable 5.1 is live for everyone. Cheaper, faster, open API.

Mythos 5.1 does the same job better, and it's locked behind a verification program. Cyber teams and life sciences labs only, and only in the US for now.

The logic makes sense. The thing that finds a vulnerability faster for you finds it faster for someone trying to do damage too.

But step back and look at the pattern. The most capable AI in the world isn't going to be the thing you download. It's going to be the thing you get vetted for.

That's a real shift, from "everyone gets the same model" to "who are you, and can we trust you with this."

Worth asking who ends up on the wrong side of that verification wall, and whether it's who you'd expect.
