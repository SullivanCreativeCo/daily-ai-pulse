LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
A model can score 99.9% on a test, and 62.7% on the exact same test, on the exact same day.

That's what happened with GPT-6 Astra this week. OpenAI ran it through their own custom harness and got 99.9% on a reasoning benchmark that had beaten every AI model before it. The people who built the actual benchmark ran their standard version and got 62.7%.

Same model. Same day. Two totally different headlines depending on who's holding the stopwatch.

I see this same thing anytime someone hands me a case study. The number is real. The conditions that produced it are the part that never makes the slide.

So when a tool, a model, or a vendor throws a big number at you, the first question isn't "is that true." It's "who ran the test, and would I get the same one."

Would someone pay for the 99.9% version, or the 62.7% version? Depends which one they actually experience.
LI . NO. 02
A guy wrote a Facebook post at 3:30 in the morning. No photos. No price. No plan for what happens if someone actually says yes.

Mike got laid off after 20 years. Sat in his garage one night and posted: woodworking birthday parties for kids. That's it. He didn't even know what age range to allow. Someone asked in the comments and he made it up on the spot.

First two parties were free, because he had zero pictures, zero reviews, nothing to point to yet. Five months later he'd cleared $50,000. This fall he signed a $30,000 contract for two hours a week teaching a homeschool co-op.

The whole thing started before it looked like a real business. That's usually the order it happens in, not the other way around.

Your ideas aren't bad ideas. They're just not finished ideas yet. Mike's wasn't finished either. He posted it anyway.
LI . NO. 03
60% of small business owners are already using AI. Almost none of them are using it on the boring part.

FreshBooks surveyed 600 of their customers this week. AI shows up everywhere in marketing, design, customer service. It barely shows up in the books. Most owners still export a report, paste it into a chatbot, and hope nothing important got left out.

FreshBooks just plugged straight into Claude instead. Ask a plain question about cash flow or open invoices, get an actual answer, no spreadsheet detour.

Here's what I keep noticing with the small business owners I work with. The flashy stuff, the content and the graphics, gets the AI treatment first because it's fun and visible. The stuff that actually eats a Tuesday afternoon, invoicing, reconciling, chasing a payment, gets ignored because it feels boring and technical.

Boring is exactly where the hours are. It's just waiting for someone to notice it's a job too.
