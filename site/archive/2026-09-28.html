Today's headline

Agents stopped answering and started doing.

Meta's Muse, an iMessage agent called szn, Alibaba's phone agents. All of them shipped with a leash: approval gates, one-time card numbers, audit trails, a runtime boundary.

The model isn't the pitch anymore. What the agent can touch, and what it can't, is.

LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
The boring part of Meta's new agent is the part that matters.

Muse can schedule meetings, order groceries, and track sales numbers. Fine. Every agent says that now.

What caught me is the guardrails. It runs in an isolated virtual machine. Logins sit in a credential store the agent can't read. At checkout it makes a one-time card number, so the merchant never sees your real one. You approve the big stuff before it fires. And there's an audit trail of everything it did.

That's the product. Not the model.

Think about who's on the other end of an agent like this. A business owner who has never once handed a stranger their login. The pitch to them isn't "it's smart." It's "here's what it can touch, here's what it can't, and here's the receipt."

Avalara's new agent hub is the same idea. Every action leaves a record an auditor can read.

Would you hand a new hire the company card on day one? Probably not. So why would anyone hand it to an agent with no receipt?
LI . NO. 02
A Canadian insurer just put a travel insurance quote inside ChatGPT.

Manulife says it's their first ChatGPT plugin anywhere. You turn it on, answer questions about your trip, and get a quote. To actually buy, it sends you over to CoverMe.com.

Read that last part again. The sale still happens on their site. ChatGPT is just where the shopping starts.

That's the shift. People don't always open a website to figure out what to buy. They ask something they already have open, and whoever shows up in that answer gets the first look.

I'm not saying every plumber needs a ChatGPT plugin. Manulife is a giant.

But the small version of this is real. When someone asks an AI about your kind of business in your town, what does it have to work with? It can only use what's written down clearly. Hours. Services. Prices. What you're good at.

Most small businesses have that scattered across five places, or nowhere.

How would I fix this? I'd write it down once, in plain words, in one place.
LI . NO. 03
Chris Koerner did something in his latest video that stuck with me.

He connected a product he'd never opened to Claude, on camera, and asked it to build the thing. He says up front he had zero experience with it.

That's the permission slip most people are waiting for.

You don't have to be the expert before you start. You have to be willing to sit with the tool and figure it out in front of someone who needs it.

His pitch is that a lot of business owners have an email list and no plan for it. His math is a plumber's list turning into $12,000 a month, with you charging $1,000 of that. Take the numbers as his, not gospel.

The part I'd actually steal is who it's for. Not you. Someone you already know who has a list sitting there.

What are you already doing for yourself that someone else would find useful?

I think most of us have one of those. We just don't count it because it only took a Tuesday afternoon.

TH . NO. 01
An ex-Apple Siri engineer is launching an AI agent that lives in iMessage. It has its own name, its own email, its own phone number. It can browse, buy stuff, and talk to vendors for you.

October launch, per Mark Gurman.

So now your vendor might get a text from your agent instead of from you.

Apple has restricted third-party stuff in iMessage before. Nobody knows yet how that goes.

I keep wondering who the vendor thinks they're talking to.
TH . NO. 02
Cactus Compute put out a 121M parameter model that does one job. Tool calling, on the device, no internet.

The binaries are 8MB to 29MB. That's small enough for wearables.

They say it beat a 1.2B model on a mobile actions test, 86.0% to 82.4%. Their numbers, so grain of salt.

Everyone's watching the giant models. Meanwhile a narrow little one is learning to work offline in your pocket.

How many of your workflows actually need a giant model?
TH . NO. 03
Alibaba's new phone agent tries the API first. If an app has a clean way to be called, the agent uses it. Tapping around the screen is the backup.

Think about what that means for a small business. If your booking software has an API, an agent can book. If it's a PDF and a phone number, the agent has to guess.

That's a real difference in who gets picked.

Is your stuff readable by something that isn't a person?
