LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
Fable 5.2 almost doubled the score on the remote labor index.

21% to 41%. One model generation.

That test uses real contractor projects. Defined work, clear finish line, someone can say yes, that's done.

Here's what I keep coming back to. The work that gets automated first isn't the hard work. It's the clear work.

If you can write down what done looks like, a model can chase it. If you can't, it wanders.

So the move isn't learning another tool this week. It's writing down what done looks like for the stuff you do every Tuesday.

The follow-ups. The quotes. The weekly report nobody reads.

If it's repeatable, it's a skill. And if it's a skill you can describe, it's a skill you can hand off.

What's one thing you do every week that you've never actually written down?
LI . NO. 02
Most people ask if they can trust the AI.

I think that's the wrong question.

Dan Clarke runs Ostina, a UK company building AI staff for small businesses. In an interview this week he put it better: draw the line around the action, not the whole system.

So you don't decide trust once. You decide it per action. What can it read? What can it draft? What can it send? Up to what amount? When does it stop and come get you?

Say it handles inbound enquiries. Fine for it to reply and update the customer record. Not fine for it to promise a price outside your range. That one comes back to you.

That's a few sentences in a doc. Not a framework.

It's how you'd onboard a person. Day one, nobody hands the new hire the company card.

Where's your line today? Is it written down anywhere?
LI . NO. 03
Anthropic just published a paper called Worse Together.

They gave every user their own AI agent, all sharing one resource. A token budget. A clinic calendar. A group food order.

Then they compared that to one agent serving everybody.

The single agent won in all four setups. By roughly 10 to 45 points.

And a lot of the agents just never did anything. As teams grew from 4 to 16 agents, the share that acted at all dropped from 66% to 10% for one model.

Here's why I care. Plenty of small businesses are being sold a sales agent, a support agent, a scheduling agent, all doing their own thing. More agents isn't more help by default.

The fix that worked for the shared budget was boring. A designated lead and clear priorities.

Who's the lead in your setup? If the answer is nobody, that's probably the bug.
