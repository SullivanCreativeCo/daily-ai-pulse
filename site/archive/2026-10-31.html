Today's headline

The wrapper around the model is where the money is moving.

Factory says its new Droid 1.0 harness did a 312-tool-call job in 2 model requests and 61,000 tokens. The normal way took 36 requests and 3.1 million. Same task. Microsoft also opened its Copilot harness to outside builders the same week.

Everybody argues about which model is smartest. The people shipping product are quietly working on what sits around it.

LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
Factory moved 151 Linear issues two ways.

The normal way took 36 model requests and 3.1 million input tokens.

The new way took two requests and 61,000 tokens.

Same job. The only thing that changed was where the work happened.

Instead of the model calling one tool, waiting, then calling the next, it writes a small script. The script runs all 312 tool calls inside a sandbox. The model only reads the final result.

Those are Factory's own numbers, so take them with a grain of salt. But the idea is what stuck with me.

We keep arguing about which model is smartest. Meanwhile the stuff wrapped around the model is cutting the token bill by about 98 percent on tasks like this.

If you build agents for a business, that's the part your client actually feels. Not the benchmark. The invoice.

Which part of your agent is paying a model to do something a script could do?
LI . NO. 02
Nvidia and Mistral both shipped open models in the same week.

Nvidia's is a 120 billion parameter model built for enterprise agents. Mistral's is a trillion parameter model with the weights now public.

Most of my clients will never run either one.

But they'll feel it anyway.

Here's why. A business owner doesn't ask what the parameter count is. They ask where their data goes. Open weights mean that answer can be "nowhere, it stays on your machine."

Nvidia's Kari Briski said it plainly: organizations can post-train on their own data, deploy on their own infrastructure, and own what they build.

That's a very different pitch than "rent our model and trust us."

For a long time the private option meant a worse option. That gap is getting smaller.

Who's the first client you'd move to a local model if the quality was close enough?
LI . NO. 03
Crewdle sells AI agents with one rule built in.

The drafts wait for a person to approve them before anything goes out.

Their sales agent researches prospects and writes outreach. It doesn't send a thing on its own. The owner reviews it first.

That's the whole reason a small business can actually say yes to this.

The pricing is interesting too. There's no per-seat fee. You add funds to a balance and pay for what runs. And if you want help, setup around one workflow is a flat CAD 2,500.

So the setup is the product. Not the model.

That's not just a service, that's a weight removal product. The owner doesn't want to learn how agents work. They want the coordination work gone.

If you're selling AI to small businesses, what's the one workflow you'd tell them to start with?
