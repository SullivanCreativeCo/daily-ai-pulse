LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
The move most people miss when they think about AI deputization: it's not about capability anymore.

Capability is table stakes. Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, SpaceX all ship capable models now. The actual question is simpler and harder: which parts of my work should I actually hand to an agent.

I listened to the AI Daily Brief this morning and they landed on five dimensions. Frequency (does it happen often and take time). Teachability (can you show the agent once). Checkability (can you verify faster than you could do it). Stakes (how bad if it goes wrong). Your involvement (does it have to be you).

Score 8-10 across those five, deputize it. Score 0-3, keep it. Score 4-7, split it.

That's it. That framework just answered the question that's been hanging since ChatGPT launched: which work actually moves to agents and which work stays human.
LI . NO. 02
Standing authenticated access. That's the inflection.

Grok Bot shipped with it. Each bot gets logged into your Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Drive, Notion. Stays logged in. Works while your laptop is closed.

In their own demo, a single bot pulled 52 Salesforce accounts, cross-referenced them against LinkedIn, pulled an analytics tool, and queued 36 outbound emails unattended.

That's not a chat experience anymore. That's an execution experience.

Most AI assistants are stateless. You ask, they answer, the session ends. Grok Bot inverts that. You create a named bot, log it in once, and it keeps working.

That changes the math on which work makes sense to deputize. Before, the agent could only help with the thinking part. Now it can handle the doing part too.

That's a different category of business.
LI . NO. 03
Frontier capability is now running on laptops.

Qwen3.8-27B just landed on LM Studio. That's Alibaba's 2.4 trillion parameter model with 95 billion active parameters. Runs locally. No server required.

The implication is the one people keep missing: you don't have to send your work to someone else's server anymore. You don't have to trust another company's infrastructure with what you're building. The capability comes to your machine.

That solves a different problem than raw speed. It solves the problem of sovereignty. Privacy. Keeping your work local.

For small teams and solo founders, that's huge. For enterprises that have been hesitant about cloud AI, that's permission granted.

The graph is decentralizing. You're looking at it happen in real time.
