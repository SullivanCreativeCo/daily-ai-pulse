LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
Every big lab shipped an agent with its own computer this week.

OpenAI has Dots. xAI has Grok Bot. Manus has Cue. Anthropic put Claude Tag in Slack.

Here's why that matters before the how.

A chatbot waits for you to type. An agent with its own computer doesn't. It has a browser, a terminal, a memory, and it keeps going after you close the laptop.

Think about a small business owner. They don't need a smarter answer box. They need somebody to chase the invoice, pull the report, and follow up with the lead who went quiet.

That's the job these products are aiming at. Not answers. Chores.

The catch is trust. Most of these are still beta, and the hard part isn't the demo. It's whether the agent only touches what it's allowed to touch.

So if an agent could take one chore off your plate today, which one would you hand it first?
LI . NO. 02
OpenAI shipped a Decisions API this week and nobody's talking about it.

It runs on a small, cheap model (GPT-6 Luna) built for one thing: making quick calls. Route this request. Sort that message. Pick the next step.

The big model only shows up when the work is actually hard.

That's the same point the AI Daily Brief made about the businesses getting the most out of AI. They build model routers.

Picture a front desk. The person at the desk doesn't do surgery. They figure out who needs to see the doctor, and how fast.

Most teams I see run everything through the most expensive model, then wonder why the bill looks like that.

One cheap model deciding. One strong model working. Boring setup, big difference.

Where in your workflow are you paying surgeon rates for front desk work?
LI . NO. 03
A $66 a month stack can run a five person company.

ChatGPT Plus at $20. Make around $12. Fireflies $10. Canva around $15. Brevo $9.

That's the lineup from one roundup this week. Writing, automation, meeting notes, design, email.

None of it is flashy. That's the point.

Meanwhile, Mega is selling managed AI marketing to small businesses. Search, ads, websites, lead follow-up, with humans behind the agents. Pricing goes through a sales call now.

So you've got two ends. Cheap tools you wire together yourself, or a service that does it for you.

In between sits the real work. Somebody has to know what a good lead looks like before software can sort one.

Would someone pay to have that part done for them? I think someone would pay for that.
