LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
A new model dropping used to feel like a threat.

Now it's the best excuse to text a client.

Here's why. If you sell the output, the model eats you. Prompt packs, thin wrappers, one-off automations. Gone by the next release.

If you sell the decision about what to fix, the model helps you. It makes the fix faster.

Corey Ganim laid this out this week. His assessments and ongoing AI help get stronger every time something ships, because clients can't tell the signal from the noise.

Mistral drops a trillion-parameter model at the end of the month. A business owner doesn't care. They care what it does for their Tuesday.

That's the job. Knowing their business well enough to say "this one thing just got easier, want me to walk you through it?"

Would someone pay for that? I think someone would pay for that.

So what are you actually selling, the output or the call?
LI . NO. 02
One in seven people in the US speaks Spanish at home.

That's 44 million people.

Now think about the last plumber, dentist or roofer you saw with a Spanish version of their video. Yeah. Me neither.

Chris Koerner walked through this on his channel. AI can now dub a video into Spanish and match the mouth to the audio. He says it takes about 20 minutes and charges around $300 a video.

The why is the whole story. The tech used to look like a bad kung fu movie, so nobody bothered. Now it looks real and the gap is just sitting there.

Your ideas aren't bad ideas. They're just not finished ideas. This one is finished. Somebody just has to knock on the door.

What's a thing you already do that a local business has never even been offered?
LI . NO. 03
Meta's new small business agent can read your Shopify orders, your QuickBooks, and your ad account.

It can also flag weird expenses and draft your customer replies.

But here's the part I'd read twice. Nothing publishes, sends or spends without you saying yes.

That approval step is the whole product. Meta even admits the agent can still get things wrong.

So the opportunity isn't the agent. It's the person who sets it up. Which tools connect, what it's allowed to touch, who checks the output.

Start with one workflow. Measure the time saved after the cleanup, not before. Then add the next one.

Do more with the same, not the same with less.

What's the one job you'd let an agent do without checking it first?
