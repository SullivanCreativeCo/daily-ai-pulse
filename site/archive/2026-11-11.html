Today's headline

Google, Anthropic and OpenAI all shipped on the same day. Carbon from Google. Sheets, Whiteboards and Data from Anthropic. Businesses in ChatGPT from OpenAI.

The one to sit with is OpenAI's. Businesses can now claim a profile inside ChatGPT, upload a product feed, and run a labeled agent that answers shopper questions. ChatGPT is quietly building its own Google Business Profile.

Right now it's US ecommerce and online services only. Local shops aren't in yet. That's the part to watch.

LINKEDIN DRAFTS

LI . NO. 01
ChatGPT just built itself a Google Business Profile.

OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta. You claim your business, get verified, and keep your info current. You can upload a product feed with pricing and availability.

Then there's the Business Agent. A shopper clicks your profile and talks to an agent that only answers from what you gave it. It's labeled, and it's separate from ChatGPT's own answer.

Right now that's US ecommerce and online service businesses only. Local shops aren't in yet.

My guess is they're next. Nobody said that. It's just how this usually goes.

The dashboard is the part I like. You see mentions and website clicks. That's it. No chats. But you can finally see whether ChatGPT is sending anybody your way.

Google Business Profile took years to matter. This one probably won't get that long.

When somebody asks ChatGPT who to call, what does it actually know about your business?
LI . NO. 02
86% of solopreneurs say they'd try AI before they'd hire anyone.

That's a Freshbooks survey, reported by Fast Company. And it tracks. AI is great at the early stuff. Scheduling. First-draft posts. A website. A rough business plan.

The number I keep thinking about is 65%. That's how many say they're feeling AI burnout.

The tool doesn't run itself. Somebody has to keep feeding it, checking it, fixing what it got wrong. That somebody is the owner. The same person we were trying to give time back to.

So the work didn't go away. It changed shape.

I don't think that's bad. It's just not free, and nobody puts it on the pricing page.

Do more with the same, not the same with less. But the more needs somebody holding the wheel.

If you set this up for a small business, who's holding the wheel in month three?
LI . NO. 03
Stop selling hours.

The Startup Ideas Podcast episode on Fable 5.2 had one idea worth stealing. The host says Anthropic's own post has the real freelance score going from 21% to 41%. I haven't checked that myself, so take it as his number.

The idea holds either way. If a model can do most of a freelance job, the hour isn't what you're selling anymore. The finished job is.

His example is a client with a 12 year old spreadsheet running the whole business. Figure out what it does. Rebuild it as a simple web app. Move the data over. Write a one page guide for the staff. Fixed price.

Would someone pay for that? I think someone would pay for that.

What makes it a real service is the honest line. What you're confident in, and what a human still has to check. Say that out loud and you're not overselling.

What's the spreadsheet a business owner you know has been scared to touch for ten years?

New harnesses & tools

  • Nod - n8n Labs A standalone personal agent that runs on your Mac or Windows machine with its own browser. It cancels subscriptions, downloads supplier invoices, watches prices, and runs on a schedule. It checks again before it sends, pays, posts or deletes anything you didn't ask for, and the model never sees your passwords. Open source, Apache 2.0, bring your own AI provider. It stops when your laptop sleeps, and there's no phone calling. A builder cares because it's an agent designed around "ask before you act," which is how you'd want one running for a client. https://blog.n8n.io/meet-nod/

  • /swarm in Google Antigravity - Google DeepMind One prompt turns into a coordinated team of agents. Carbon takes the architecture, coordination and auditing roles, lighter models do the parallel work, and adversarial verifiers test every change. Worth trying on a real build to see whether the verifier step catches anything a single agent would miss. https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-carbon-now-available/

  • Arca - GlobeNewswire A desktop app that pays you for your old AI sessions. It finds your Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and Claude Desktop history, strips sensitive details on your device, and pays weekly for approved ones. Open to everyone today. Think about what's actually in your sessions before you upload anything. Client names and half-finished contracts live there. https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/11/3400565/0/en/arca-opens-to-everyone-paying-people-for-the-ai-sessions-they-d-otherwise-throw-away.html
