Anthropic spent the summer asking 900,000 small business owners what they wanted from AI.

Not bookkeeping. Not scheduling. Not the stuff everyone assumed AI would fix first for a small business.

That tells you something about where the actual pain lives. Owners aren't drowning in admin as much as they're drowning in leads they never followed up on and marketing they never got around to.

So Anthropic shipped 43 new workflows and 27 integrations this week. Shopify, Salesforce, TikTok Ads, the works.

Here's the thing worth sitting with though. You don't need Anthropic's tool to act on this insight. You need the insight.

What's the one follow-up your business keeps dropping? What are you already doing for yourself that a customer would pay for if you just packaged it?
LI . NO. 02
There's a new AI model out this week called Jev. It doesn't chat with you. It doesn't write essays. It just makes decisions.

Feed it structured data, it spits out a typed answer. That's it. No conversation, no personality, no chat window.

The guy who built it used to help invent RLHF, the technique that made ChatGPT talk like a person in the first place. Now he's building the opposite bet on purpose.

Most of us think of AI as a thing you talk to. That's the ChatGPT mental model and it's stuck in everyone's head.

But a huge chunk of the AI that's actually going to run your business quietly in the background doesn't need to say a word. It just needs to sort the ticket, route the call, flag the weird transaction.

Would someone pay for a tool like that? I think someone would pay for that. The best automations are usually invisible.

What's a decision in your business that gets made the same way, every single time, and nobody's automated it yet?
LI . NO. 03
Meta just rolled out a subscription ladder for creators and small businesses. $2.99 a month at the bottom. $499 at the top.

Scheduling. Shared logins. An AI agent that answers your WhatsApp Business messages while you sleep.

None of it is a bad idea on its own. The problem is nobody adds up the total.

A year ago "using AI in your business" mostly meant a free ChatGPT account and a little curiosity. Now it means a stack of monthly subscriptions creeping toward a real number, and most owners aren't tracking it because each individual charge feels small.

This isn't a scolding post. I'm not here to tell you you're behind.

I'm saying: before you add the next tool, ask what it's actually replacing. Do more with the same, not the same with less. If a $50 tool doesn't remove a task you were paying someone (including yourself) to do, it's not a tool, it's a subscription you forgot about.

What's on your stack right now that you couldn't explain the ROI on if someone asked?
